## 2. The decision the plan could not make: gate against VG10

The plan was written 2026-08-14 and specified `BivariateModelDefinition` as the parent. VG20's promotion on 2026-08-19 made that ambiguous, and the ambiguity is not cosmetic, because **VG19 and VG20 are not composable as written**.

Each child's effects form a covariance matrix. VG20's is 2x2 over `(delta_u, delta_q)` — the two outcomes' constant offsets — with one correlation, `rho_uq` = +0.368 [0.287, 0.447]. VG19's is two separate 2x2s, over `(b0_u, b1_u)` and `(b0_q, b1_q)` — each outcome's own offset and rate. The union is a **4x4 with six correlations**:

| #   | correlation                                | VG20         | VG19      | full 4x4  |
| --- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------ | --------- | --------- |
| 1   | `b0_u`-`b1_u` (offset-rate, comprehension) | n/a          | estimated | estimated |
| 2   | `b0_q`-`b1_q` (offset-rate, production)    | n/a          | estimated | estimated |
| 3   | `b0_u`-`b0_q` (cross-outcome offsets)      | **`rho_uq`** | 0         | estimated |
| 4   | `b1_u`-`b1_q` (cross-outcome **rates**)    | n/a          | 0         | estimated |
| 5   | `b0_u`-`b1_q`                              | n/a          | 0         | estimated |
| 6   | `b1_u`-`b0_q`                              | n/a          | 0         | estimated |

Carrying `rho_uq` across while leaving 4-6 at zero was rejected for two reasons. First, `rho_uq` would stop meaning what it means in VG20: there `delta_u` is the child's entire understood effect, here `b0_u` is only their effect **at 36 months**, so publishing the same name would invite a comparison that is not like-for-like. Second, and decisively, **row 4 is the scientifically interesting one** — do children who gain comprehension faster also convert faster? — and setting it silently to zero while estimating row 3 would repeat, one level up, exactly the assumption VG20 exists to correct. VG10's independence assumption is on record as "contradicted by its own fitted deviations".

**Decision (study owner, 2026-08-21): gate VG19 against VG10.** VG19 and VG20 are parallel refinements of the same parent. `BivariateChildSlopeModelDefinition` therefore subclasses `BivariateModelDefinition`, so `subject_re_correlation_eta` is not a field VG19 can set; `_resolve_subject_re_correlation` refuses the combination as defence in depth. G4 becomes a clean one-factor LOO against VG10.

The cost is stated plainly: the winner of that comparison is not the model of record, and reconciling VG19 with VG20 is deferred. Doing it properly needs its own Gate 1 on whether rows 4-6 are identified at all — which is doubtful, since the drift evidence is measured on the spoken proportion and spoken is `p_u * q`, so the likelihood cannot attribute drift to either curve. The plan already predicts the two `tau1`s will be individually poorly identified; correlations _between_ poorly identified quantities are not a promising starting point.

## 3. Two engine subtleties worth recording

**The predictive branch order.** `_child_slope_block` is checked **before** `_subject_scales`. A slope model also has an age-varying between-child SD, so it would otherwise take the A1 branch — which scales a single deviate by that curve and imposes perfect rank correlation across age. Children would never cross, which is precisely the constraint the slope exists to relax. Same curve, different children. Verified: VG20's unseen-child variables are unchanged (`_delta_subj_u_marg`, `_z_subj_q_marg`) while the slope path emits four scalar deviates.

**A bug caught in review.** The first version of `child_spread_product`'s guard tested `tu.ndim == 2` to detect an age-varying scale. After the shape adapter a _constant_ scale is also 2-D — `(n_draw, 1)` — so the test was always true and would have raised on legitimate VG20 calls. It now tests the caller's input.

## 4. The age-varying spread

Under a slope the between-child spread is a curve, not a number: `sqrt(tau0^2 + 2 rho01 tau0 tau1 D + tau1^2 D^2)` at `D = (age - ref)/12`. `comparison.child_scale_of_age` computes it and both `child_spread_single` and `child_spread_product` accept a `(n_draw, n_age)` scale. Checked against a 400,000-draw Monte Carlo from the same Cholesky block, and against the two nested cases — `tau1 = 0` reproduces the constant-offset model at every age, and `sd(ref_age) = tau0` exactly.

It is a parabola in age with its minimum at `D = -rho01 tau0 / tau1`, so a negative `rho01` puts the tightest point in the future and has children fanning out on both sides of it. That is a qualitative claim the constant-offset model cannot make, and it should be read off the figure rather than assumed.

## 5. What registering cost, beyond the registry entry

The inventory's checklist lists six obligations. Registering VG19 tripped **four separate pinned tests**, each of which exists to make a silent change visible, and one of them was not on the checklist:

1. `test_model_definitions.py` — the kappa-form list. VG19 inherits VG10's two-anchor form.
2. `test_clamp_scope.py` — `CLAMP_Q_ONLY_MODELS`.
3. `test_trend_gp_consolidation.py` — the engine dispatch, which fails with a bespoke message rather than the misleading "Model has not been set in the context".
4. **`test_a1_subject_scale.py::test_no_model_of_record_carries_an_age_varying_scale`** — not on the checklist. It asserted every registry subject-scale field is a plain float, as a catch-all behind the A1 guard.

Number 4 deserved thought rather than a quick edit, and the distinction it forced is worth stating: **A1 is inadmissible in a model of record precisely because it pins `rho01 = 1`**, forcing children never to cross, against a measured rank correlation of about 0.75-0.83 to two years and 0.28 beyond. A child slope **estimates** `rho01`, so A1 is its special case and the slope frees the constraint rather than imposing it. The guard now admits a `SubjectSlopePriorParams` and still rejects an `AgeVaryingSubjectScale`.

Also updated: `regenerate_plots.ENGINE_BY_MODEL` (without it a cosmetic plot fix silently requires a refit), the model inventory, and the three synced instruction files — verified byte-identical afterwards.

## 6. Post-registration review, 2026-08-21

A read-through of the VG19 and VG20 code paths after registration, before any fit. The algebra was checked independently rather than through the tests: the 2x2 Cholesky gives `Var(b0) = tau0^2`, `Var(b1) = tau1^2`, `Cov = rho01 tau0 tau1`, and `child_scale_of_age` matches a two-million-draw Monte Carlo to better than 0.1% including the negative-`rho01` case where the parabola's minimum sits in the future. Three things worth recording as confirmed rather than assumed: `X_obs` reaches `build_child_slope` as raw months, so the rate really is per year of real age; `ref_age = 36` is the pool's **exact** median observation age (35.4 on understood alone), so the centring is real and not a round number; and VG20's `rho_uq` is carried correctly by `comparison.py`'s derived spoken scale, keyed off the definition field, with the unguarded call in `compare_ds_td_re.py` being inside `_verify()` where the independent path is deliberately exercised against an independent Monte Carlo.

Two defects, both fixed here.

**The age-varying spread was plumbed but not connected.** `child_scale_of_age` had **no callers**. `subject_heterogeneity` still loaded `tau_subj_u` / `tau_subj_q` as scalars, and because VG19 emits `tau_subj_u` as a Deterministic equal to `tau0`, that load _succeeds_ — it would have silently reported the 36-month spread at every age from 8 to 115 months, discarding `tau1` and `rho01`. This is the #224 defect class exactly: a fitted parameter thrown away by the derived quantity that exists to use it, with the comment saying so about `rho_uq` sitting three lines above. It was not a live wrong number, because `DS_KEY` is `vg20` and VG20's scale genuinely is constant; it was a trap primed to fire the moment VG19 was fitted and compared, including in G4's own between-child diagnostic, and it feeds a published table. Fixed by keying off the definition's scale field the way `rho_uq` already is, and building the curve **after** the caller's grid is resolved rather than interpolating it from the model's native grid — `_prepare` was split so the scale is no longer carried through the grid step. Verified byte-identical on VG20 and VG11 against the pre-change code, and covered by three new tests including one that fails if the scale collapses to `tau0`.

**G3 could not have run.** VG19 was in neither `_TARGETS` nor `UNSUPPORTED_REASONS`, so `fit_recovery.py vg19` failed with the generic "no recovery specification registered" — the entry §5's plan calls for, missed at registration. Added as `_BIVARIATE_RE`, which is correct unchanged: the simulator samples the observation nodes from the real graph at a fixed truth draw, so the slope is carried without a spec change, and the scoring classifier already handles the new variables (the three scalars are picked up by dimension, `*_rho_raw` is excluded by the `*_raw` rule, and the two-dimensional `*_z` falls through both branches rather than erroring).

**And one new gate, G5b** — see the [implementation plan](202608141900-child-slope-implementation-plan.md). `tau1`'s leverage is concentrated in the sparse high-age tail: 50 spoken rows above 84 months are 3.5% of the data and carry 28-35% of the cross-sectional information about it, and the prior's implied between-child SD at 115 months puts 27% of its mass above 4 logits against 0.8% at 36. Since `tau1` is global, that leverage propagates back into the dense, reported range. The check is a refit capped at 84 months. This is a different risk from the funnel the plan already names — influence rather than geometry — and it was not on the gate list.
