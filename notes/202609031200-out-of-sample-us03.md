## Headline

**The conversion model transfers to an unseen study. The comprehension model does not, and the way it fails is specific: the study's departure from the fitted curve grows with age, and the model has no term that can absorb an age-varying study effect.**

## What was validated before anything was concluded

The reconstruction of the model outside PyMC is exact. `expit(f_u_plot)` equals `p_u_plot` and `expit(h_plot)` equals `q_plot` to zero error; `p_s_plot` equals their product to zero error; `delta_u` equals `delta_u_raw * tau_u` and `delta_subj_u` equals its raw times `tau_subj_u`, confirming which scale is between-study and which between-child. Interpolating `f_u_plot` and taking the median reproduces the published `posterior_summary_u.csv` reference child to within 0.6 words at every age from 12 to 60 months.

On fitted studies of comparable size to this one, at 100 rows or more, the estimator's error never exceeded **0.07**, so the whole-study estimate below is reliable to about that.

## The comprehension failure is outside the model's own null distribution

Twenty-four studies were simulated from the model itself, carrying us_03's exact ages and repeat-visit structure, each with a study offset drawn from the fitted `tau_u` of 0.36:

| Statistic over 24 simulated studies | Min   | Median | Max   | us_03 |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- | ------ | ----- | ----- |
| 89% coverage, understood            | 0.822 | 0.895  | 0.937 | 0.798 |
| Median PIT, understood              | 0.260 | 0.492  | 0.738 | 0.356 |
| 89% coverage, spoken conditional    | 0.704 | 0.965  | 0.997 | 0.944 |

**No simulated study reached us_03's comprehension coverage.** Nor is a low offset sufficient to produce it: the seven simulated studies whose true offset was at or below -0.3 returned coverage of 0.822 to 0.899. Reducing the draw count to match the simulations moves us_03 to 0.784, further out, so the gap is not a Monte Carlo artefact.

The median PIT tells the other half of the story. At 0.356 it sits comfortably inside the simulated range, so the **location** shift is entirely within what a constant study offset explains. It is the **tails** that fail.

## Two alternative explanations, tested and rejected

**The 396-item form.** The models score every count against an 810-item reference, so a study wholly on the shortest form could read low for that reason alone — the `dse-native-only` question. It does not explain this. Restricting to the 180 rows where under 5% of the predictive mass sits above 396 gives 89% coverage of 0.800 and median PIT 0.350, against 0.794 and 0.362 for the other 107 rows. More directly, `us_01` is the same 396-item Words and Gestures form in the fitted pool and carries a fitted offset of **+0.15**. Across the fourteen fitted studies the correlation between offset and form length is only +0.27.

**A young sample outrunning the curve's calibration.** Rejected on the age table above: the study matches the reference child where it is youngest and diverges as it ages, which is the opposite of what this would predict.

## Three defects found in my own work, all fixed

1. **The offset estimator was wrong.** It first took the mean of each row's continuity-corrected empirical logit residual and returned **-0.96, outside the fitted range**, which would have been reported as a failure of the reference-child estimand. Thirty of 287 rows record zero comprehension; `logit` is undefined at zero and the `+0.5` correction places those rows near -5.5, supplying more than half the displacement. It is now a profile likelihood with the child effect integrated out by Gauss-Hermite quadrature. **Any summary that logit-transforms individual counts is unusable on a source with a floor, and this pool has several.**
2. **No missing-outcome guard.** The fitted Down syndrome pool carries 448 spoken-only rows on the fallback likelihood. The first calibration control ran on all 1,424 and returned 0.631 coverage, because a comparison against `NaN` reads as a miss. Restricted to the 972 complete rows the same code returns 0.920. The script now drops incomplete rows and reports the count. The `us_03` frame has none, so its numbers never changed.
3. **A mislabelled diagnostic column.** `observed_t1` in the within-child output selected its column by testing whether `"understood"` appeared in the outcome name, which is true of `spoken_given_observed_understood`. Reporting only; no coverage figure was affected.

## Three models, not one, and they are indistinguishable

VG09, VG10 and VG20 were all scored against the same held-out study. They share an engine and differ in the child structure: VG09 and VG10 give a child independent comprehension and conversion effects (VG10 adds the anchored dispersion), and VG20 adds the correlation between them, estimated at 0.39.

| Model | Marginal cover89, understood | Offset, understood | Second visit cover89, spoken | Second visit PIT, spoken |
| ----- | ---------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| VG09  | 0.798                        | -0.60              | 0.865                        | 0.415                    |
| VG10  | 0.791                        | -0.60              | 0.865                        | 0.437                    |
| VG20  | 0.798                        | -0.56              | 0.846                        | 0.466                    |

Every conclusion above holds for all three. The comprehension failure is not an artefact of VG20's correlation, and neither is the conversion success.

**The correlation does not pay out of sample.** Held-out log predictive density for the second visit, summed over 104 children, with paired differences against VG20:

| Comparison                                                  | Total | Per child | z     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------- | ----- |
| VG20 minus VG09, conditioning on both visit-1 outcomes      | +1.14 | +0.011    | +0.34 |
| VG20 minus VG10, conditioning on both visit-1 outcomes      | +0.81 | +0.008    | +0.25 |
| VG20 minus VG09, conditioning on visit-1 comprehension only | -2.85 | -0.027    | -0.86 |
| VG20 minus VG10, conditioning on visit-1 comprehension only | -2.91 | -0.028    | -0.87 |

The second pair is the sharper test: predicting a child's later production from their earlier comprehension alone is precisely the task the correlation exists to perform, because conditioning on visit-1 production identifies the conversion effect directly and leaves the correlation little to do. On that task VG20 is marginally **worse**, though not distinguishably so.

This is worth stating carefully. It does not show the correlation is wrong; parameter recovery confirmed it is identified, and it is the estimand behind the comparison claims about which children convert more. It shows that on 104 unseen children it buys no predictive gain, and that the in-sample model comparisons, which favour richer structures by construction, overstate what it does for a new cohort. With 104 children a per-child gain below about 0.06 nats would be invisible here.

## What this means for the ingestion refit

The model has a constant per-study offset on comprehension. This study's departure is not constant; it is near zero at 17 months and about one logit by 34 months. Two readings remain open and this study alone cannot separate them: the population comprehension curve rises too steeply between 20 and 35 months for every study, or study effects are genuinely age-varying and the model needs a term for that. The first is testable once this study is in the pool, since the curve will move; the second is a structural question and connects to Proposal A1's age-varying scale, already registered as a sensitivity variant on VG10.

Ingesting the study will pull the comprehension curve down between 20 and 35 months and widen the between-study scale. Both are wanted. Neither was predictable from the fitted pool, which is the argument the out-of-sample test was built to make.

## Caveats

- One study is one draw from the between-study distribution. Every row shares its study effect, so the 287 rows are not 287 independent tests. The simulation-based null distribution is the honest reference and is what the comprehension conclusion rests on, not a binomial standard error.
- The offsets are point estimates at the posterior mean of the curve and the scales. The profile interval covers the offset's own uncertainty, not the curve's.
- Twenty-four simulated studies bound the null distribution loosely. Nothing here should be quoted as a p-value beyond "outside 24 of 24".
- The three rows from the structurally distinct older sub-sample (62–80 months, near the form ceiling) survive the exclusion and sit in the sparsest band. Too few to matter, but not from the same recruitment as the rest.
- Three models were scored. VG19 and VG22 were not: they are the only registered models with **age-varying child structure**, which is exactly what this study's age-varying departure calls for, but an unseen child's effect under them is age-dependent and the script draws a single time-constant pair. That is the first extension to make.
- VG02, the univariate Down syndrome comprehension model, was not scored either. It would separate whether the comprehension failure belongs to the comprehension curve itself or to the bivariate joint structure. It runs on a different engine and needs a variable-name adapter.
- VG16 is deliberately excluded: its conversion predictor is a function of the child's own earlier comprehension, so predicting an unseen study means simulating the lag chain rather than evaluating a likelihood. This is the same property that excludes it from parameter recovery.
- Scoring more models against the same study adds evidence about **model structure**, not about the study. The comprehension finding rests on one cohort however many models are scored against it.
