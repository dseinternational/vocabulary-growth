**The conversion model transfers to an unseen study. The comprehension growth curve does not.**

## Two alternative explanations, both tested and rejected

**The 396-item form.** The models score every count against an 810-item reference, so a study wholly on the shortest form could read low for that reason alone — the `dse-native-only` question. It does not explain this. Restricting to the 180 rows where under 5% of the predictive mass sits above 396 gives 89% coverage of 0.800 and median PIT 0.350, against 0.794 and 0.362 for the other 107 rows. More directly, `us_01` is the same 396-item Words and Gestures form in the fitted pool and carries a fitted offset of **+0.15**, so this instrument does not force a negative offset. Across the fourteen fitted studies the correlation between offset and form length is only +0.27.

**A young sample outrunning the curve's calibration.** Rejected on the same evidence: the study matches the reference child at 17–19 months, where it is youngest, and diverges as it ages.

## A defect in the first version of this analysis

The study offset was first estimated as the mean of each row's continuity-corrected empirical logit residual, and returned **-0.96, outside the fitted range**, which would have been reported as a failure of the reference-child estimand. It was an artefact of the estimator. Thirty of 287 rows record zero comprehension; `logit` is undefined at zero and the `+0.5` correction places those rows near -5.5, supplying more than half the displacement. Excluding them gave -0.43 and the median residual was -0.37. The estimator is now a profile likelihood over a grid of candidate offsets with the child effect integrated out by Gauss-Hermite quadrature, which treats a zero count as the ordinary Beta-Binomial event it is, and returns -0.56 with a 95% profile interval. The lesson generalises: any summary that logit-transforms individual counts is unusable on a source with a floor, and this pool has several.

## Against the pass criteria set in advance

Issue #288 fixed the criteria before the data were seen. Scoring honestly:

- **89% coverage within about ±4 points of nominal, both outcomes, inside the fitted window** — **not met**. Understood is 9 points low; spoken is 5 points high marginally, though within tolerance conditionally at 0.846.
- **Study offsets inside the fitted spread** — **met**, both quantities.
- **Conditional coverage no worse than marginal** — **met for spoken** (0.846 against a nominal 0.89, versus 0.944 marginally, so closer to nominal), **not met for understood** (0.740 against 0.798).
- **No systematic sign to predicted minus observed** — **not met for understood**. The model over-predicts comprehension at every age above 20 months, and over-predicts the growth between the two visits.

## What this means for the ingestion refit

The comprehension miss is a real finding and it is the first time any of these models has been scored against data it did not see. Three readings are open and this study alone cannot separate them: the population comprehension curve rises too steeply between 20 and 35 months; or the between-study scale `tau_u` is too small to accommodate a study this far out, which the narrow 89% intervals would also explain; or this cohort genuinely differs and the model is right to be surprised once. The second and third are distinguishable once the study is in the pool, since `tau_u` will move if it was too small.

Ingesting this study will pull the Down syndrome comprehension curve down between 20 and 35 months and will widen the between-study scale. Both are wanted. Neither was predictable from the fitted pool, which is the argument the out-of-sample test was built to make.

## Caveats

- One study is one draw from the between-study distribution. Every row shares its study effect, so the 287 rows are not 287 independent tests and the marginal coverage figures carry far more uncertainty than a binomial standard error would suggest. The study-offset test in the table above is the honest statement of the same evidence.
- The three rows carrying a structurally distinct older sub-sample (62–80 months, near the form ceiling) survive the exclusion and sit in the sparsest age band. They are too few to matter but they are not from the same recruitment as the rest, per the source documentation.
- The offsets are point estimates at the posterior mean of the curve and the scales, not a joint posterior. The profile interval covers the offset's own uncertainty, not the curve's.
- VG20 alone was scored. VG10, VG19 and VG22 carry different child structures and #288 asks for the same comparison across them; that has not been run.
