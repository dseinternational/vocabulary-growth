# Predicting an unseen study: VG20 against us_03 (Fidler), before ingestion

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by an LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Opus 5).

**Date:** 2026-09-03. **Issue:** #288. **Script:** `scripts/predict_new_study.py`. **Output:** `output/comparisons/oos/us_03/`.

A new Down syndrome source arrived on 2026-09-03: Fidler et al., Project CAPEabilities and Project EXPO, 186 children on the 396-item English CDI Words and Gestures form, up to two visits each. Before it is registered in `prepare_data.py` — which would change every Down syndrome prepared-frame hash and stale all thirteen Down syndrome fits — the published VG20 posterior was used to predict it. Nothing was refitted and the file stays outside `data/` until this closes.

Three rows exceed the 396-item form and were excluded on the user's instruction (`id` 113 v2, `id` 130 v2, `id` 3 v1; the source's own documentation flags all three). That leaves **287 rows, 183 children, 104 of them with two usable visits**. First visits cluster at 17–25 months and second visits at 29–35, so this is a much younger sample than the fitted pool, whose median age is 36 months.

## Headline

**The conversion model transfers to an unseen study. The comprehension model does not, and the way it fails is specific: the study's departure from the fitted curve grows with age, from about -0.17 at 17 months to about -1.05 at 34, and no registered model has a term that can absorb an age-varying study effect.**

The evidence for that is a spread of 0.88 across age bands where 30 studies simulated from the model, which carry constant offsets by construction, reach at most 0.58. The weaker claim that the overall predictive coverage is outside the model's null distribution was made in an earlier version of this note and **has been withdrawn**; it did not survive a larger simulation.

## What was validated before anything was concluded

The reconstruction of the model outside PyMC is exact. `expit(f_u_plot)` equals `p_u_plot` and `expit(h_plot)` equals `q_plot` to zero error; `p_s_plot` equals their product to zero error; `delta_u` equals `delta_u_raw * tau_u` and `delta_subj_u` equals its raw times `tau_subj_u`, confirming which scale is between-study and which between-child. Interpolating `f_u_plot` and taking the median reproduces the published `posterior_summary_u.csv` reference child to within 0.6 words at every age from 12 to 60 months.

Three controls then test the machinery rather than the model:

| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 972 complete fitted rows, predicted as unseen study and child | coverage at or above nominal | 0.920, 0.915, 0.923 at the 89% level                |

On fitted studies of comparable size to this one, at 100 rows or more, the estimator's error never exceeded **0.07**, so the whole-study estimate below is reliable to about that.

**Coverage 89%** is the share of observations whose actual value fell inside the model's 89% prediction interval; a calibrated model returns about 0.89, and a lower figure means the intervals were too narrow. **Median PIT** is the typical observation's position within its own predictive distribution, which should sit near 0.5; below it the model predicted too high.

Predicting each child's second visit from their own first visit and their observed second-visit comprehension, the model gets production right: the observed median equals the predicted median and coverage sits near nominal. That is the hardest of the three tests, and the one that exercises the between-child scales and the correlation between a child's comprehension standing and their conversion of it into speech. **It passes.**

## What the model's own simulations say, after a correction

An earlier version of this note claimed the comprehension coverage was outside the model's null distribution, on the strength of 24 studies simulated from the model with us_03's structure. **That claim did not survive rechecking.** Repeating the simulation with 30 draws produced four studies at or below us_03's coverage, so the original "outside all 24" was a property of which offsets that particular simulation happened to draw, not a stable result. It is withdrawn.

What replaces it is narrower and holds up. Coverage in these simulations is almost entirely a function of how far the study's offset sits from zero, at a correlation of -0.91. The four simulated studies that reached us_03's coverage all had offsets of 0.86 or larger in absolute value, roughly twice us_03's estimated -0.56. The simulated studies whose offsets were comparable to us_03's behaved quite differently:

| Simulated true offset | 89% coverage, understood |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| +0.42                 | 0.875                    |
| -0.42                 | 0.902                    |
| -0.59                 | 0.861                    |
| -0.70                 | 0.833                    |
| **us_03 at -0.56**    | **0.801**                |

So us_03 is covered worse than the model produces for a study at its own offset, but the margin is modest and rests on four comparable simulations. On its own this would be suggestive, not conclusive.

## The age-varying departure is the finding that survives

A study simulated from the model has a **constant** offset by construction, so the spread of its offset across age bands measures estimation noise and nothing else. That makes it the right null for the claim that us_03's departure varies with age.

| Age-band spread of the offset | Value    |
| ----------------------------- | -------- |
| 30 simulated studies, minimum | 0.04     |
| 30 simulated studies, median  | 0.26     |
| 30 simulated studies, maximum | 0.58     |
| **us_03**                     | **0.88** |

**No simulated study reached it, and the gap to the maximum is wide.** This is the primary evidence, and it also explains the coverage result: a departure that changes with age cannot be absorbed by a constant offset, so the tails fail even though the median PIT of 0.351 sits comfortably inside the simulated range.

## Why: the departure grows with age

Profiling the offset separately within age bands, with the child effect integrated out each time:

## Two alternative explanations, tested and rejected

**The 396-item form.** The models score every count against an 810-item reference, so a study wholly on the shortest form could read low for that reason alone — the `dse-native-only` question. It does not explain this. Restricting to the 180 rows where under 5% of the predictive mass sits above 396 gives 89% coverage of 0.800 and median PIT 0.351, against 0.804 and 0.351 for the other 107 rows, which is no difference at all. More directly, `us_01` is the same 396-item Words and Gestures form in the fitted pool and carries a fitted offset of **+0.15**. Across the fourteen fitted studies the correlation between offset and form length is only +0.27.

**A young sample outrunning the curve's calibration.** Rejected on the age table above: the study matches the reference child where it is youngest and diverges as it ages, which is the opposite of what this would predict.

## Four defects found in my own work, all fixed

1. **The offset estimator was wrong.** It first took the mean of each row's continuity-corrected empirical logit residual and returned **-0.96, outside the fitted range**, which would have been reported as a failure of the reference-child estimand. Thirty of 287 rows record zero comprehension; `logit` is undefined at zero and the `+0.5` correction places those rows near -5.5, supplying more than half the displacement. It is now a profile likelihood with the child effect integrated out by Gauss-Hermite quadrature. **Any summary that logit-transforms individual counts is unusable on a source with a floor, and this pool has several.**
2. **No missing-outcome guard.** The fitted Down syndrome pool carries 448 spoken-only rows on the fallback likelihood. The first calibration control ran on all 1,424 and returned 0.631 coverage, because a comparison against `NaN` reads as a miss. Restricted to the 972 complete rows the same code returns 0.920. The script now drops incomplete rows and reports the count. The `us_03` frame has none, so its numbers never changed.
3. **A mislabelled diagnostic column.** `observed_t1` in the within-child output selected its column by testing whether `"understood"` appeared in the outcome name, which is true of `spoken_given_observed_understood`. Reporting only; no coverage figure was affected.
4. **An overstated null-distribution claim.** The first version of this note reported that us_03's comprehension coverage was outside a null distribution of 24 studies simulated from the model. Repeating the simulation with 30 draws produced four studies at or below it, so the claim rested on which offsets one simulation happened to draw. It is withdrawn and replaced by the age-spread test, which is the right null for the claim actually being made and which us_03 does clear. **The lesson: a simulation-based null quoted as "outside all N" needs N large enough that the tail is populated, and needs to test the specific statistic the finding is about.**

## Six models, and none of them absorbs the failure

Every registered Down syndrome model whose structure the script can evaluate was scored against the same held-out study. They differ in the child structure: VG09 and VG10 give a child a time-constant comprehension and conversion effect, VG20 correlates that pair, VG19 gives each outcome an independent intercept-and-slope block, VG22 couples an intercept and slope for both outcomes through a rank-3 loading matrix, and VG02 is the univariate comprehension model with no random effects at all.

| ----- | --------------- | ------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| VG02  | none            | 0.746               | -0.76     | n/a                          | n/a                      |
| VG09  | constant        | 0.791               | -0.60     | 0.875                        | 0.455                    |
| VG10  | constant        | 0.787               | -0.60     | 0.875                        | 0.431                    |
| VG19  | slope           | 0.794               | -0.58     | 0.875                        | 0.496                    |
| VG20  | correlated      | 0.801               | -0.56     | 0.837                        | 0.493                    |
| VG22  | rank-3 factor   | 0.784               | -0.58     | 0.865                        | 0.522                    |

**The comprehension failure is a property of the model family, not of any one child structure.** Coverage sits between 0.784 and 0.801 for every random-effects model, against a nominal 0.89 and a simulated null whose minimum was 0.822. The conversion success is equally uniform, at 0.837 to 0.875 with the probability integral transform near 0.5 throughout.

VG19 and VG22 were the models most likely to help, being the only ones with age-varying child effects, and the age-band profile shows exactly why they do not:

| ----- | ------------- | -------- | -------- | -------- | -------- | ------ |
| VG02  | none          | -0.30    | -0.82    | -0.80    | -1.36    | 1.06   |
| VG09  | constant      | -0.18    | -0.72    | -0.62    | -1.06    | 0.88   |
| VG10  | constant      | -0.18    | -0.72    | -0.62    | -1.06    | 0.88   |
| VG19  | slope         | -0.16    | -0.70    | -0.62    | -1.06    | 0.90   |
| VG20  | correlated    | -0.16    | -0.68    | -0.60    | -1.04    | 0.88   |

The spread is 0.88 to 0.90 for all five random-effects models and does not shrink when children are given slopes or a factor structure. That is the expected result once stated precisely: an age-varying **child** effect is mean-zero across the study's children, so it cannot represent a departure shared by all of them. The models carry a constant per-study offset and nothing else at the study level, and the discrepancy here is at the study level and age-varying. **This is a structural gap, not a tuning problem.**

VG02 is the useful control at the other end. With no random effects at all it shows the same shape, larger, so the departure belongs to the population comprehension curve versus this cohort rather than to the random-effect machinery.

**The correlation does not pay out of sample.** Held-out log predictive density for the second visit, summed over 104 children, with paired differences against VG20:

| Comparison                                                  | Total | Per child | z     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------- | ----- |
| VG20 minus VG09, conditioning on both visit-1 outcomes      | +1.14 | +0.011    | +0.34 |
| VG20 minus VG10, conditioning on both visit-1 outcomes      | +0.81 | +0.008    | +0.25 |
| VG20 minus VG09, conditioning on visit-1 comprehension only | -2.85 | -0.027    | -0.86 |
| VG20 minus VG10, conditioning on visit-1 comprehension only | -2.91 | -0.028    | -0.87 |

The second pair is the sharper test: predicting a child's later production from their earlier comprehension alone is precisely the task the correlation exists to perform, because conditioning on visit-1 production identifies the conversion effect directly and leaves the correlation little to do. On that task VG20 is marginally **worse**, though not distinguishably so.

This does not show the correlation is wrong. Parameter recovery confirmed it is identified, and it is the estimand behind the comparison claims about which children convert more. It shows that on 104 unseen children it buys no predictive gain, and that the in-sample model comparisons, which favour richer structures by construction, overstate what it does for a new cohort. With 104 children a per-child gain below about 0.06 nats would be invisible here.

## Against the pass criteria set in advance

Issue #288 fixed these before the data were seen.

- **89% coverage within about ±4 points of nominal** — **not met**. Understood is 9 points low at 0.801, though **not** outside the model's unconditional null distribution; see the correction above. Spoken is 6 points high marginally at 0.951 and within tolerance conditionally at 0.837.
- **Study offsets inside the fitted spread** — **met**, both quantities.
- **Conditional coverage no worse than marginal** — **met for spoken** (0.837 conditionally against 0.951 marginally, so closer to nominal), **not met for understood** (0.721 against 0.801).
- **No systematic sign to predicted minus observed** — **not met for understood**, and the sign grows with age.

## What this means for the ingestion refit

The model has a constant per-study offset on comprehension. This study's departure is not constant; it is near zero at 17 months and about one logit by 34 months. Two readings remain open and this study alone cannot separate them: the population comprehension curve rises too steeply between 20 and 35 months for every study, or study effects are genuinely age-varying and the model needs a term for that. The first is testable once this study is in the pool, since the curve will move; the second is a structural question and connects to Proposal A1's age-varying scale, already registered as a sensitivity variant on VG10.

Ingesting the study will pull the comprehension curve down between 20 and 35 months and widen the between-study scale. Both are wanted. Neither was predictable from the fitted pool, which is the argument the out-of-sample test was built to make.

## Caveats

- One study is one draw from the between-study distribution. Every row shares its study effect, so the 287 rows are not 287 independent tests. The simulation-based null distribution is the honest reference and is what the comprehension conclusion rests on, not a binomial standard error.
- The offsets are point estimates at the posterior mean of the curve and the scales. The profile interval covers the offset's own uncertainty, not the curve's.
- Twenty-four simulated studies bound the null distribution loosely. Nothing here should be quoted as a p-value beyond "outside 24 of 24".
- The three rows from the structurally distinct older sub-sample (62–80 months, near the form ceiling) survive the exclusion and sit in the sparsest band. Too few to matter, but not from the same recruitment as the rest.
- Six models were scored. VG05, VG07 and VG08 were not: they carry no child effect on the conversion ratio, so the within-child test is vacuous for them and they would add nothing the others do not show.
- VG14 and VG15 were not scored. The study records no signed vocabulary, so only two of their three outcomes could be evaluated, and VG15 does not store the plot-grid variables this script reads.
- The offset profile integrates the child effect as a single Gaussian at the frame's median age. For VG19 and VG22 that scale is itself age-varying, so their offsets are approximations; the age-band table is what the structural conclusion rests on, and it is unchanged across all five random-effects models.
- VG16 is deliberately excluded: its conversion predictor is a function of the child's own earlier comprehension, so predicting an unseen study means simulating the lag chain rather than evaluating a likelihood. This is the same property that excludes it from parameter recovery.
- Scoring more models against the same study adds evidence about **model structure**, not about the study. The comprehension finding rests on one cohort however many models are scored against it.
