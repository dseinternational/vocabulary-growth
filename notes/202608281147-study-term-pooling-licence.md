# Why the study-level terms license pooling the gesture measure with the sign sources — and where the licence stops

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by an LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5).

Analysis record, 28 August 2026. [202608271551](202608271551-es01-gesture-construct.md) established that `es_01`'s non-vocal column measures the symbolic-gesture repertoire rather than taught signing, and asserted that `delta_psi` "is what licenses pooling a gesture measure with sign measures at all". This note verifies that claim against the built graph in [`src/vocab_growth/models/common_joint_modality.py`](../src/vocab_growth/models/common_joint_modality.py) and states exactly what the study-level terms guarantee, what they do not, and what follows for reporting. Every code claim below was checked against the current implementation on 2026-08-28. It also corrects one overstatement made in the review discussion of the same date: that the sign sources' per-study associations are "essentially untouched" by `es_01`'s presence (§4.4).

## 1. What pooling asserts without a study term

Until 2026-08-12, `log_psi` was a single global scalar. A shared scalar asserts **row-level exchangeability**: every cross-tab row, from every source, is treated as governed by the same association. Under that assumption the posterior for $\psi$ is approximately a precision-weighted average over sources — each source pulls on the shared parameter in proportion to the information its rows carry. The consequence is composition sensitivity, and it was measured, not hypothesised: $\psi$ moved 1.80 → 2.49 when `uk_07`'s 82 rows arrived ([202608120030](202608120030-uk07-pactds-integration-and-ds-refit.md)), and admitting `es_01` — 185 of the 434 $\psi$-informing rows, sitting at independence — would have dragged the headline toward 1 by row weight. In that regime, pooling a gesture measure with sign measures genuinely corrupts the estimand: the reported $\psi$ is whatever mixture of constructs happens to be in the pool.

## 2. What the graph actually does (verified)

Six structural facts, each checked in the current `common_joint_modality.py`:

1. **Both Dirichlet-Multinomial likelihoods take the per-study association.** `log_psi_obs = log_psi + delta_psi[study_codes]`, and the four-cell DM (`cells_obs`) and the within-produced DM (`nz_prod_cells_obs`) both exponentiate `log_psi_obs` at their own rows. No cell of any source is ever scored against another source's association.
2. **`delta_psi` is zero-summed over the $\psi$-informed studies only** — the studies contributing four-cell (`idx_cells`) or produced-cell (`idx_prod`) rows — and pinned to exactly 0 elsewhere, so uninformed studies cannot counterbalance the informed ones. `tau_psi ~ HalfNormal(1.0)` scales the offsets; the `ZeroSumNormal` sigma is rescaled by $\sqrt{K/(K-1)}$ so the marginal per-study prior variance stays $\tau^2$.
3. **The DMs also take per-study margins.** The cell probabilities are built from `q_obs_pop` and `r_obs_pop`, which include `delta_q` and `delta_sign` and exclude only the subject random effects — the `_pop` suffix means population-of-children, not population-of-studies. So a systematic level difference in the gesture margins (the gesture lexicon runs to a few dozen items where sign vocabularies run to hundreds — mean signed 50.7 for `es_01` against 81–187 for the six sign sources at 34–56 months, [202608271551 §3](202608271551-es01-gesture-construct.md)) lands in `delta_sign[es_01]` and `delta_q[es_01]`, not in the shared latent trajectories.
4. **`es_01` is sign-informed through its cells.** `sign_informed` unions the studies with signed marginals and the cross-tab studies, so `es_01` carries a `delta_sign` coordinate even though its four-cell rows set the marginal `signed` to NaN.
5. **The reported composition is a population quantity on both axes.** The reporting deterministics (`pi_both_*`, `pi_sign_only_*`, `pi_speak_only_*`, `pi_neither_*`, `p_any_*`) use the population $\psi$ (no study shift) and the population margins `r_plot`/`q_plot`/`p_u_plot` (no study shift). Per-study associations are reported separately as `psi_study`.
6. **The study terms are age-constant intercepts.** The signed age profile is one shared latent (the three-anchor tent plus GP); `delta_sign` adds a constant on the logit scale, so per-study curves are parallel by construction.

## 3. The licence, stated exactly

Three guarantees follow, and the first two are algebraic identities of the graph rather than asymptotic hopes.

**(a) Likelihood-level separation.** `es_01`'s cells are only ever evaluated against $\psi_{es01}$ and its own shifted margins; `uk_02`'s cells never see a parameter that `es_01`'s rows enter directly, and vice versa. Gesture data cannot masquerade as sign data anywhere in the likelihood. Each source's per-study association is therefore an estimate of what _that source_ measures — which is what makes `psi_study[es_01]` readable as a gesture–speech association and the others as sign–speech associations, per [202608271551 §3](202608271551-es01-gesture-construct.md).

**(b) Fixed, bounded influence on the headline.** Because `delta_psi` sums to zero over the $K$ informed studies, the identity $\log\psi = \frac{1}{K}\sum_s \log\psi_{study,s}$ holds **exactly, per draw**: the population $\psi$ is the geometric mean of the per-study associations, each study weighted $1/K$ regardless of how many rows it contributes. A source's rows influence the headline only through where its own per-study value sits; adding rows cannot raise its weight in the mean. Consistency check against the dev-config fit of [202608121030 §6](202608121030-psi-heterogeneity-and-age-invariance.md): the geometric mean of the per-study medians (1.08, 2.31, 3.38, 3.67) is 2.36, which is the reported population value to two decimals; the same arithmetic without `es_01` gives 3.06. So `es_01`'s claim on the headline is one-quarter of a log-scale mean — a known, fixed weight — against the ~43% row share it would carry under pooling. (Medians do not compose exactly through a nonlinear identity, so the two-decimal agreement is consistency, not proof; the per-draw identity is the proof.) The same construction holds for the margins: the population $\text{logit}\, r(a)$ is, per draw, the equal-weight mean of the sign-informed studies' levels at every age.

**(c) Commensurability is estimated, not assumed.** $\tau_\psi$ is the dial between the two regimes. At $\tau \to 0$ the offsets vanish, every per-study value collapses onto `log_psi`, and the common value is again fitted by all cells jointly — row-weighted pooling; this is exactly why the registry describes `tau-psi-narrow` as forcing "near-pooling (roughly the pre-2026-08-12 behaviour)". At large $\tau$ each per-study value sits where its own cells put it and the centre is their plain mean. The fitted `tau_psi` (0.654, [0.233, 1.176], dev config) is bounded away from zero, and the dedicated simulation study ([202608242030](202608242030-psi-hierarchy-simulation.md)) found `HalfNormal(1.0)` well calibrated at this design (per-study coverage 0.885 against nominal 0.89) while `tau-psi-narrow` destroys the calibration of the parameter it acts on (coverage 0.022). So the data get to decide how far the gesture study is pooled with the sign studies, and they decide: not far.

## 4. Where the licence stops

Each limit below is verified in the graph or already on record; together they are why the construct finding still has reporting consequences even though the machinery is right.

**4.1 The centres are cross-construct mixtures, so naming is on us.** The reported composition and `p_any` (§2.5) are built from the population $\psi$ and population margins — under [202608271551](202608271551-es01-gesture-construct.md), centres over three sign measures and one gesture measure. The hierarchy makes these quantities composition-_stable_ (a new source enters at weight $1/K$, not row weight); it cannot make them _mean_ "signing". Reporting `pi_sign_only` from the population $\psi$ as a signing quantity would quietly average a gesture–speech association into a sign–speech headline. This is the estimand-naming decision raised in the 2026-08-28 review: the signing findings should read the sign-taught stratum's per-study values (or a centre over them), with `es_01` presented as the gesture counterpart.

**4.2 Levels are protected; shapes are not.** The study terms are age-constant (§2.6), so the zero-sum bounds `es_01`'s pull on the _level_ of $r(a)$ at every age — but the age _shape_ (tent plus GP) is shared and fitted by row-weighted likelihood pull. Where the gesture source is a minority of many, the shape damage is diluted; where it is the only evidence, nothing dilutes it. `es_01` supplies 52 of the 142 signed observations below 24 months and is the only $\psi$-informing source below about 19 months ([202608271551 §4](202608271551-es01-gesture-construct.md), [202608121030 §4](202608121030-psi-heterogeneity-and-age-invariance.md)) — and the gesture trajectory has its own documented shape (early rise, plateau at 18–21 months mental age, decline as speech supplants it). At the young end, the shared signed shape and the young-age association evidence are gesture evidence, and no term in the graph can tell the model otherwise. This is the argument for registering a sensitivity that removes `es_01`'s signed evidence entirely (marginal and cells), which `include_es01_cells = False` does not do — that flag returns the rows to the merged view, where the gestured totals re-enter $r(a)$ as ordinary signed marginals.

**4.3 Under-recovered $\tau_\psi$ leaks row weight back toward pooling.** The per-draw identity fixes the headline as the mean of the per-study values, but shrinkage decides where those values sit. If $\tau_\psi$ recovers low, the per-study values are dragged toward a common compromise that is row-weighted — and `es_01` is the largest source, so the leak runs toward the gesture value, biasing the population $\psi$ (and every per-study value) downward. The general recovery evidence points this way ([#226](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/226); the report caveats state it as the between-study scale under-estimated and the group estimates over-shrunk). The targeted simulation ([202608242030](202608242030-psi-hierarchy-simulation.md)) bounds it: at the information the real cross-tabs carry, per-study over-shrinkage does **not** reproduce (reaching it needs the sources eight times less informative), and the bias that does reproduce is $\psi$ low by 3.3% from its own prior. So the leak is real in direction, small in measured size, and catastrophic only under a deliberately narrow $\tau$ prior — which is what the `tau-psi-narrow` variant is registered to show.

**4.4 Cross-study coupling exists at the prior level — the correction.** The 2026-08-28 review discussion said the sign sources' estimates are "essentially untouched" by whether `es_01` is in the pool. Too strong. The likelihood-level separation (§3a) is exact, but two prior-level channels remain: `es_01` moves the centre (2.36 against ≈ 3.06 by the §3b arithmetic), and shrinkage pulls every per-study value toward that centre; pulling the other way, `es_01` widens $\tau_\psi$ (the observed spread includes it), which loosens the shrinkage. The net movement of `uk_02`/`uk_07`/`nz_01`'s per-study values is an empirical question, and the measurement already exists: the `psi-drop-es01` variant, fitted at `rep` on 2026-08-14 ([202608142000 §7.1](202608142000-refit-run-record-and-disk-failure.md)) — subject to `compare_sensitivity.py`'s stale-pairing check against the current VG15 baseline before it is quoted.

**4.5 The hierarchy inherits the cell likelihood's recorded limits unchanged.** $\psi$ is _decoupled_ from the child sign effects by construction rather than empirically separated from them, and repeated cross-tab visits by one child are conditionally independent in the cell likelihood, so children with more visits weigh more and $\psi$'s uncertainty may be understated ([#238](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/238), [202608231536 §2](202608231536-vg14-vg15-statistical-review.md)). The study terms neither cause nor cure any of this.
