# VG20 with a constant sex shift: what the exploratory arm returned

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by an LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5.1).

**Date:** 2026-09-04. **Issue:** [#295](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/295). **Follows:** [`202609041206`](202609041206-sex-differences-in-vocabulary.md), which measured the girl-boy difference descriptively and recommended one exploratory variant of VG20 rather than a change to the model of record. **Script:** `scripts/experiments/vg20_sex_arm.py`. **Output:** `output/vg20-sex-arm/` (a separate output root; the comparison tables are under `comparisons/sex-effect/`). **Tier:** `test` (4 chains × 2,000 draws after 2,000 tuning).

## Headline

**Inside VG20's structure the sex difference is real, small, and sits on the production ratio more than on comprehension.** Girls are ahead of boys by 0.18 logits [89% ETI 0.06, 0.30] on words understood and by 0.36 [0.16, 0.56] on the production ratio, the share of understood words that are spoken. Both intervals exclude zero. The second is twice the first, with a posterior probability of 0.91 that it is the larger; that is the expressive-not-receptive shape the Down syndrome literature describes, and it reverses the emphasis of the descriptive regression, which had put the larger effect on comprehension once ie_02 was included. Against the between-child spread the shift is a quarter of a child standard deviation on comprehension and 0.29 on the production ratio, and it removes 1% and 2% of the between-child variance. `rho_uq`, the child scales, the study scales and the population trajectories do not move. In words on the population curve, girls lead boys by about 35 understood words from 3 years on and by 19 spoken words at 36 months, 46 at 48 and 60 at 60 to 72 months.

**The recommendation of the earlier note stands: not in the model of record, reported in the discussion as heterogeneity, now with a fitted number behind it.** The note's own threshold for revisiting the model of record, a change in the child scales or the production ratio larger than their interval widths, is not approached: the largest movement in any population quantity is 5% of the control's interval width.

## What was fitted

Three arms, each VG20's registered definition rebuilt onto `BivariateSexShiftModelDefinition`, an unregistered sibling subclass in `definitions.py` that the bivariate random-effect engine reads through `getattr`. With neither of its two fields set the subclass builds VG20's graph exactly, fingerprint and log-probability, which `tests/test_sex_shift_variant.py` pins, and the graph-equivalence suite confirms no registered model moved. Every arm ran through the full pipeline into its own output root, so each carries a manifest, a prepared-frame hash, the predictive checks and the convergence gate, while remaining unregistered and unpublishable.

| Arm       |  Rows | Children | Studies | Model                                                                                                                                          | Gate                                                          |
| --------- | ----: | -------: | ------: | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `control` |   997 |      559 |       8 | VG20 restricted to administrations with a recorded sex                                                                                         | REVIEW: 1 divergence, R-hat 1.012 on two HSGP coefficients    |
| `sex`     |   997 |      559 |       8 | The same rows plus `beta_sex_u`, `beta_sex_q` ~ Normal(0, 0.5) on a girls +½ / boys −½ contrast, on the understood and production-ratio logits | REVIEW: 0 divergences, R-hat 1.017 on three HSGP coefficients |
| `full`    | 1,424 |      763 |      14 | VG20 on every row, through the inert subclass                                                                                                  | PASS                                                          |

The sex-known subset is es_01, ie_02, uk_01, uk_02, uk_05, uk_06, uk_07 and us_01: 259 girls and 300 boys, 451 girl and 546 boy administrations, 719 with words understood and 994 with words spoken. The three studies the earlier note listed but this database had been missing (ie_02, uk_05, uk_06) are present because the DuckDB database was rebuilt from the recoded CSVs first; the main checkout's database predated the recoding of #292 and still returns no sex for them.

The two REVIEW verdicts are the HSGP coefficients at the `test` tier, as for every hierarchical model at this tier; every parameter this note reports has R-hat at or below 1.006 and bulk ESS above 900. Sampling took 3 minutes 10 seconds for each subset arm and 5 minutes for the full pool.

The contrast coding matters for reading the tables. With girls at +½ and boys at −½ the population curve is the sex-balanced average, so the `sex` arm's `*_query` outputs are comparable with the control's, and each coefficient is the girl-minus-boy difference in logits. The prior carries about 2% of the posterior precision on `beta_sex_u` and 6% on `beta_sex_q`; a flat prior would return roughly 0.18 and 0.38.

The fitted comprehension effect lands on the typically developing value and on what six of the seven Down syndrome studies gave individually, not on the pooled +0.33 that ie_02's +1.09 pulled upward; the child and study random effects do what the descriptive study dummies could not. The production-ratio effect comes out larger than either descriptive figure. The descriptive regression read the ratio off `spoken / understood` on the same administration before the loader's masking, with no child effects; here `q` is the model's conditional production ratio, estimated on the paired likelihood with the child's comprehension standing absorbed by `delta_subj_u`. The spoken shift, which is what both descriptive references measure most precisely, agrees across all three columns. The spoken shift on the population curve is 0.50 logits at 24 months and 0.30 at 72, falling as the production ratio approaches one, because a fixed shift on the ratio's logit contributes less to spoken words once most understood words are already spoken.

The child scales fall by the amount a balanced binary covariate of this size should remove, β²/4: 1.1% of the between-child variance on comprehension and 1.7% on the production ratio, against 1.5% and 2.1% expected from the point estimates. The correlation is unchanged. The population curves at the canonical ages differ between the control and sex arms by at most 2.5 words, 1% to 5% of the control's 89% interval width, and the production ratio by at most 0.004.

The `full` column is the cost of the restriction, and it is not small. The six studies without a sex column (ie_01, it_01, nz_01, uk_03, uk_04, us_02) have lower comprehension curves and add child heterogeneity: the full pool's population understood curve sits 20 to 37 words below the sex-known subset's at 24 to 60 months, 0.3 to 0.8 of the subset's interval width, its spoken curve 2 to 12 words below at 24 to 48 months and level by 72, and its production ratio lower at 24 to 36 months and higher by about 0.035 at 60 to 72. Neither sex arm's absolute curves are VG20's, therefore. The girl-boy contrast is estimated within the subset and is what transfers.

## Is the shift constant in age?

The check the earlier note asked for, residual means by sex within age band, has to be done with a **new-child** predictive. The pipeline's stored posterior predictive conditions on each child's fitted random effect, and those effects absorb most of any child-level covariate: under the control model, with no sex term at all, it already reproduces 14 of the 19 spoken words by which girls lead boys, so it cannot test the term. `vg20_sex_arm.py compare` therefore also replicates every administration from the population curve at its age, its study effect and a fresh child effect pair drawn from the fitted between-child distribution, one pair per child, with the paired spoken structure kept (`vg20_sex_arm_ppc_marginal_by_sex.csv`). Over all ages the control cannot reproduce the observed girl-boy difference on either outcome (z of 1.9 and 2.4) and the sex arm reproduces both (z of −0.1 and 0.2). For words understood every band is inside the sex arm's predictive. For words spoken the constant shift leaves two adjacent bands about two standard deviations out in opposite directions: at 30 to 42 months the observed gap of 42 words is larger than the 19 the model predicts, and at 42 to 54 months girls are 23 words behind boys where the model predicts 23 ahead. Neither is study composition. Within the 30 to 42 band girls lead boys inside every study that contributes both sexes (es_01 122 against 59 spoken words, ie_02 33 against 6, uk_01 115 against 50, uk_02 105 against 66, uk_05 58 against 39), a within-study weighted difference of 46 words, with the two sexes' mean ages equal to within 0.2 months; within the 42 to 54 band boys lead inside every study but ie_02 (es_01 191 against 150, uk_01 168 against 140, uk_02 268 against 246, uk_07 143 against 86), a within-study difference of −27 words. This is the same peak-then-dip that the earlier note's by-age descriptive estimates showed (+1.0 logits at 30 to 42, −0.04 at 42 to 54) and called noise. With the replicated distributions in hand it reads as two deviations of about two standard deviations among ten band tests, in opposite directions, which is more than chance expects but not a trend: a linear age-by-sex term, the next step the earlier note named, could not fit a rise followed by a reversal, and a sex-specific curve would be estimating a shape from 200 and 145 administrations. The constant shift is the right summary of what the pool supports. If sex is ever pursued further, the first thing to check is who the 42 to 54 month administrations are: whether the boys observed there are a longitudinal follow-up of an abler subgroup, which the child random effects would absorb, rather than boys catching up.

## Predictive comparison

The paired leave-one-out comparison between the two subset arms is not usable. Sex minus control is +2.5 (se 2.4) for words understood and −8.5 (se 5.4) for words spoken, but 202 to 235 of the pointwise Pareto k values in each arm exceed 0.7, which is the known failure of importance-sampled LOO under per-child random effects: leaving out one administration of a singleton child asks the sampler to re-estimate that child's effect, and it cannot. A covariate that removes 1% to 2% of between-child variance is not expected to show in a predictive score in any case. A leave-child-out score would be the honest test and was not run.

## What it tells us

1. **The effect survives the model.** With child and study random effects in place, girls remain ahead of boys, on comprehension by the size the typically developing norms show, on the production ratio by rather more.
2. **It is a statement about the study's own estimand.** The production ratio, the quantity the report describes as the share of understood words a child says, differs by sex by about 0.36 logits, nine percentage points at 48 months. That is the finding worth a sentence in the discussion: girls with Down syndrome convert more of their comprehension into speech, which is the expressive pattern the literature reports, and it is not explained by their comprehension advantage.
3. **It does not change what the report reports.** Population trajectories, the production ratio by age, `rho_uq` and the child scales are unmoved; the covariate explains 1% to 2% of between-child variance; a fixed shift of a quarter of a child standard deviation cannot narrow a child-level predictive interval appreciably. The reasons the earlier note gave for keeping sex out of the model of record all hold, and the restriction cost measured here adds one: a model with sex loses six studies and a quarter of the children, and its population curves are visibly different ones.
4. **Constant in age is adequate but not perfect on spoken words.** The overall shift is right; two adjacent bands disagree with it in opposite directions, consistently across studies. Worth knowing, not worth a sex-specific curve.

## Caveats

`test` tier, one fit per arm, with the HSGP coefficients under the gate's R-hat and ESS thresholds as they are for every hierarchical model at this tier; the coefficients of interest converged cleanly, and a `rep` fit would tighten nothing that matters here. The arms are exploratory: fitted through the full pipeline but unregistered, so their output validates against nothing and must not be synced or published. The subset is 559 of VG20's 763 children, and its population curves differ from the full pool's by up to 0.8 of an interval width, so nothing here is a statement about VG20's absolute trajectories. Sex on parent-report instruments may carry reporter expectation as well as real difference, as the earlier note says. The new-child predictive check draws one child effect per child and 400 posterior draws with a fixed seed; the band cells range from 30 to 994 administrations, and the two smallest bands cannot say much. The girl-boy contrast is symmetric about a pool that is 54% male, so the "pooled" curve in the tables is the balanced average, 0.01 logits above the pool's own.

## Reproduce

```bash
uv run python scripts/prepare_data.py   # the database must postdate #292
uv run python scripts/experiments/vg20_sex_arm.py --output-dir output/vg20-sex-arm fit control
uv run python scripts/experiments/vg20_sex_arm.py --output-dir output/vg20-sex-arm fit sex
uv run python scripts/experiments/vg20_sex_arm.py --output-dir output/vg20-sex-arm fit full
uv run python scripts/experiments/vg20_sex_arm.py --output-dir output/vg20-sex-arm compare
```

The `full` arm is optional and the slowest; `compare` runs without it. Every table in this note is one of the six CSVs `compare` writes.
