## Parameter recovery

Run after the publication tail, at `test` tier against the headline set (VG20, VG12, VG15), three replicates each, truth drawn from each model of record's own posterior. Nine fits, all completed; the scoring pass wrote `output/comparisons/recovery/` and the tables are staged into `docs/comparison/`.

**The tier is the limiting factor, not the models.** The harness assesses a replicate only if max R-hat is at or below 1.01 and minimum ESS at or above 400, and carries divergences as caveats. On that rule three of nine were assessed: VG20 one of three (R-hat 1.0116 / 1.0053 / 1.0136), VG12 two of three (1.0060 / 1.0064 / 1.0114, with 17 / 13 / 18 divergences), VG15 none of three (1.0152 / 1.0176 / 1.0198, and no divergences at all). VG20 needing `rep` to converge in recovery was already known; **VG15 needing it is new**, and its three clean-but-under-mixed replicates are the clearest statement of it. A `rep`-tier VG20 run was queued behind this one and is in flight; VG15 has not been rerun.

**VG20's headline parameters recover.** In the one assessed replicate the child-effect correlation comes back at 0.436 against a truth of 0.441 (89% [0.366, 0.503], z = -0.12), and both between-child scales are inside their intervals (`tau_subj_u` z = -1.45, `tau_subj_q` z = -1.60). The fourteen study offsets correlate 0.89 (understood) and 0.90 (production ratio) with their truths, at 10/14 and 11/14 interval coverage against a nominal 12.5. Overall 110 of 131 targets are inside their 89% intervals. The two scalar misses are mild (`slope_q` z = -2.01, `p_slope_hi_q` z = -1.80). This is the first direct evidence that the correlation the Down syndrome / typically developing comparison rests on is identified rather than prior-driven, and it answers #233's observation that the validation checks never exercised the correlated-effect structure.

**VG12 reproduces the #225 between-child bias.** `tau_subject` returns below its truth in all three replicates (-5.3%, -8.1%, -7.3%; z = -2.97, -4.82, -4.12), outside the 89% interval every time, while the variance budget `v_total` recovers cleanly (z = +0.42, +0.13, +0.82). The mis-estimate is in the split (`subject_variance_share`, z = -2.19, -2.87, -2.88), which the square-root transformation then amplifies. Interval coverage over all targets is 24/40 and 17/40 in the two assessed replicates, which is poor, but the targets are strongly correlated functions of one GP and this is not simulation-based calibration.

**A stale claim in the comparison book was corrected.** The book asserted in static prose that the between-child scale "comes back low in nine of nine replicates across three models". That is the #225 baseline cycle's reporting-tier evidence, and this cycle does not reproduce it: `tau_subj_u` comes back _high_ in VG20 r01 (+2.3%) and VG15 r01 (+0.3%), so the tally here is 7 of 9, spanning -8.1% to +2.3%. The sentence is now a computed cell that tallies the staged replicates, separates the ones the gate assessed (3 of 3 low) and attributes the nine-of-nine figure to the cycle that produced it. The cancellation argument it supports is unaffected, because that argument turns on the bias being shared by both sides rather than on its exact size.

**Two things to fix before the next recovery run.** The comparison book's VG12 cell reads every `recovery_vg12_r*.csv` regardless of the gate verdict, so it currently reports "3 replicates of 3" while the harness assessed two; the count should be gate-aware. And the DS-side bias it uses for the correction arithmetic is hard-coded at -5.81% from an earlier cycle rather than read from the staged VG20 tables.
