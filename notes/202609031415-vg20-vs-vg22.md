# VG20 against VG22: the better description and the weaker basis for publication

Written because the comparison had never been made in one place. The two models are the last two entries in the Down syndrome joint lineage, they differ in exactly the part of the model the reported between-child contrasts read, and the material for comparing them was spread across two fit directories, an out-of-sample run and a recovery matrix.

## Headline

**VG22 is the better description of these children and the weaker basis for publication today.** It fits production markedly better in sample, predicts it better out of sample, is immune to a conditioning pathology VG20 has, and estimates a level-to-rate coupling that is the most interesting child-level result in the family. It also has no parameter-recovery evidence at any tier, an unresolved rank, five undesigned correlation priors, and a leave-one-out advantage its own diagnostics undermine. VG20 stays the model of record. VG22 is the successor to promote once recovery has run, and that run belongs early in the post-ingestion queue rather than late.

Nothing here is a reason to revisit any published population number: the two models' trajectories are interchangeable at every reported age.

## Structure, and the nesting

VG22 is the strict generalisation. VG20 gives each child a time-constant offset on comprehension and another on the conversion ratio, coupled by one free correlation `rho_uq`. VG22 gives each child a **level and a rate** on both outcomes — four effects — drawn from one rank-3 factor, `b = Lz` with a free `(4, 3)` loading matrix, evaluated at a 36-month reference age. **VG20 is VG22 with both rate loadings at zero.** Neither VG10 nor VG19 is nested in VG22 at any rank below 4, for the reason `docs/models/vg22/index.qmd` records: two independent per-outcome blocks are a full-rank matrix with cross terms at zero, and a factor form reaches zero cross-correlation only by giving the outcomes orthogonal directions, which at rank 3 it cannot do for all four effects at once.

## These two fits are prior-comparable, and the inventory says otherwise

`docs/models/README.md`'s VG22 row states that "every fitted number quoted in this row predates that redesign and was sampled under the arcsine", the redesign being #283's designed prior on the implied level–level correlation. That was true when it was written. It is **not** true of the fit of record: this fit's manifest records `subject_factor` = `{rank: 3, ref_age_months: 36.0, rho_uq_eta: 2.0, tau1_q_sigma: 0.5, tau1_u_sigma: 0.5}`, so `rho_uq` carries the exact LKJ(2) form VG20 places on its own, and the two models' correlations are estimated under the same prior. The row's numbers are stale and its caveat now misdirects; refreshing it is a small job and is listed in the refit issue.

The five other implied correlations are still induced rather than designed, and are uniform — the LKJ(1) marginal, `P(|rho| > 0.8) = 0.20`. That is stated in VG22's own report and is unchanged.

## Where they differ: the child level

| Quantity                                    | VG20                 | VG22                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| `rho_uq`, the level–level correlation       | 0.390 [0.314, 0.463] | 0.311 [0.218, 0.400] |
| Comprehension level spread (`tau_subj_u_0`) | 0.781 [0.735, 0.830] | 0.757 [0.709, 0.805] |
| Ratio level spread (`tau_subj_q_0`)         | 1.348 [1.262, 1.438] | 1.251 [1.166, 1.341] |
| Comprehension **rate** spread               | not in the model     | 0.094 [0.042, 0.147] |
| Ratio **rate** spread                       | not in the model     | 0.718 [0.620, 0.818] |

Every level quantity comes back smaller under VG22, and the reason is mechanical: the rates absorb variance VG20 has no choice but to load onto levels. The ratio rate spread is large and excludes zero decisively — children with Down syndrome differ substantially in _how fast_ their production share moves, not only in where it sits — and VG20 cannot express that at all. The comprehension rate spread is small but also excludes zero.

`rho_uq` dropping from 0.390 to 0.311 under a matched prior is the same effect seen from the other side. Both intervals exclude zero comfortably, so the qualitative claim behind the reported comparison — children ahead on comprehension convert more of it — survives either parameterisation. The magnitude does not: about a fifth of VG20's correlation is coupling that belongs to the rates.

## What only VG22 estimates

Its implied 4x4 correlation matrix (`subject_factor_corr.csv`, at the 36-month reference age):

| Pair                                         | Implied correlation     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| comprehension level ↔ ratio **rate**         | 0.393 [0.265, 0.518]    |
| ratio level ↔ ratio rate                     | 0.385 [0.260, 0.503]    |
| comprehension rate ↔ ratio rate              | 0.625 [0.209, 0.894]    |
| comprehension level ↔ ratio level (`rho_uq`) | 0.311 [0.218, 0.400]    |
| comprehension level ↔ comprehension rate     | -0.249 [-0.560, +0.094] |
| comprehension rate ↔ ratio level             | 0.448 [-0.021, +0.842]  |

The first row is the substantive result, and it is what Gate 1 predicted before the model was built: **a child's comprehension standing predicts their conversion _rate_ more strongly than it predicts their conversion _level_** — 0.393 against 0.311, on the same fit, under the same reference age. VG20 estimates only the weaker of the two and has nowhere to put the stronger.

Three of the six cross terms have intervals spanning or nearly spanning zero, all three involving the comprehension rate, whose spread is the smallest of the four. That is the identification limit the rank choice is about, not a defect.

**That difference should not be acted on, and the reason is in the same table.** VG22 spends 135 more effective parameters on the spoken outcome. More seriously, 477 of its 1,409 spoken rows have a Pareto shape above 0.7 (405 bad plus 72 very bad) against VG20's 337 (300 plus 37) — so a third of VG22's estimate rests on importance weights the diagnostic declares unreliable, and the relative efficiency behind the standard error is 1.29 against VG20's 1.02. A 195-nat gap is large enough that it probably survives the caveat, but nothing here establishes that.

**And there is no paired comparison.** `compare_models`/`loo_compare` has never been run on this pair, so the difference above has no standard error attached: it is two independent elpd totals differenced by hand, which ignores the covariance between them. The existing `loo_compare_ds_bivariate_re_*` outputs cover the random-effect contrast, not this one. Running the paired comparison is a cheap job and is in the refit issue.

Indistinguishable on coverage, and identically unable to absorb the study's age-varying comprehension departure — which is the expected result, because that failure is at the study level and both models carry only a constant per-study offset.

Paired per-child log predictive density for the second visit, 104 children, positive favouring VG20:

| Quantity                                     | Total | Per child | z     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------- | ----- |
| Spoken, given both visit-1 outcomes          | -4.34 | -0.042    | -1.66 |
| Spoken, given visit-1 comprehension only     | -1.97 | -0.019    | -0.77 |
| Understood, given both visit-1 outcomes      | +2.19 | +0.021    | +0.80 |
| Understood, given visit-1 comprehension only | +6.36 | +0.061    | +2.89 |

**VG22 predicts later production better; VG20 predicts later comprehension better.** Neither production difference is distinguishable from zero at 104 children, and the comprehension one is.

One asymmetry is not a matter of degree. Giving VG20 the visit-1 **production** count in addition to the comprehension count makes its visit-2 **comprehension** prediction _worse_, by 4.14 nats (z -1.8), concentrated in the fifteen children who produced a fifth or more of what they understood at visit 1 (-0.13 nats each against -0.03 for the rest). VG22 shows +0.03 total on the same contrast, and VG09 -0.08: no such loss. VG20's single correlation has to read a high production ratio as evidence of high comprehension standing, and on a study whose ratios are high because of its own conversion offset that inference is wrong. VG22's rates absorb it instead. **More information making a prediction worse is a specification fault, not sampling noise**, and it is the one thing in this comparison that counts against VG20 on its own terms.

## The decisive asymmetry: validation

VG20 has parameter recovery at `rep` with all three replicates assessed by the gate, `rho_uq` inside its 89% interval in each (truths 0.312/0.441/0.329 against posteriors 0.342/0.436/0.379), and both between-child scales recovered. **VG22 has never been through recovery at any tier.** Until it has:

- its four scales and six implied correlations are unvalidated, and five of those correlations carry induced uniform priors rather than designed ones;
- the rank is unsettled — rank 2 and rank 3 disagree on the spoken child-rate scale by about 2.8 combined standard errors on shared data, and #281 lists re-selecting the default rank as outstanding;
- the level-to-rate coupling that is the model's headline result has no evidence that this data size can identify it, which is exactly the question recovery answers.

That is not a small gap for a model whose distinguishing parameters are all in the block recovery would test.

## What would settle it

Four jobs, in order of what they decide.

1. **VG22 parameter recovery at `rep`**, three replicates, `--truth posterior`. If the rate scales and the level-to-rate correlation recover, the case for promotion is close to made. If the rate scales come back biased the way VG12's `tau_subject` does (#225), the extra structure is not trustworthy at this data size.
2. **Re-select the rank** on the refitted family, which #281 already requires.
3. **A paired `loo_compare`** of VG20 against VG22, so the 205-nat difference has a standard error and the Pareto diagnostics are reported beside it.
4. **The out-of-sample scores repeated against the refitted models** after `us_03` is in the pool, since the current ones use posteriors that predate the study and 104 children is thin.

## Caveats

- Both fits predate `us_03`'s ingestion, so both go stale on it. Every number here is a comparison of the two models as published on 2026-09-03, not a prediction about how they will compare afterwards. VG22 gains a study with 104 repeat-visit children, which is exactly the design its rates are identified from, so the comparison could move materially.
- The leave-one-out difference is unpaired, and a third of VG22's spoken rows carry unreliable Pareto shapes. Read it as a direction, not a magnitude.
- The out-of-sample log predictive densities are over 104 children, where a per-child difference below about 0.05 nats is invisible. Only the comprehension-only conditioning contrast clears that.
- `rho_uq` is comparable between these two fits under the matched `eta = 2`, and is **not** comparable with any VG22 number published before #283 or quoted in the inventory's VG22 row.
- Nothing here bears on VG19, which is a parallel refinement rather than a step in the same line, and which is not nested in VG22.
