# uk_01 Words & Sentences checklist total: 690 → 680

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by an LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5).

## Summary

The `survey_vocab_max` recorded for uk_01's CDI Words & Sentences (WS) rows — hard-coded as 690 in `research-data-analysis`'s `prepare/uk_01_edg.py` since that script's creation, and briefly written as 689 in `data/vocab_data_uk_01.md` — is wrong. The true WS vocabulary checklist total is **680**, the standard MacArthur CDI: Words and Sentences count. This note records the evidence, the correction, and the measured impact: the prepared-frame hash changes for exactly eight models (VG07–VG10, VG16, VG19, VG20, VG22), whose fits of record are thereby invalidated; every other registered model's prepared frame is byte-identical before and after.

## Evidence

**The original project report.** `project_2.doc` (August 2000, Sarah Duffen Centre; DSE research archive, `Research/dse-backup-sdc-docs-research/Projects/MacArthur/`, alongside `Combined groups.sav` and the `MacArthur2.xls` data-entry workbook) is the study write-up: 218 children with Down syndrome aged 1–9 years, 154 WS + 64 WG administrations, supervised by Prof. Sue Buckley. It describes administering "the MacArthur Communicative Development Inventory" with standard instructions, scoring "in accordance with the instructions in the manual" (Fenson et al., 1993), and comparing results against the Fenson normative sample — no restructured UK form is mentioned. Its instrument paragraph states the WS scale "consists of a checklist of 689 words … includes 396 words from the Words and Gestures scale". That sentence is the traceable source of the 689 that stood in `data/vocab_data_uk_01.md` from 2026-07-13 to 2026-08-31 — but it is itself an error: the same paragraph says the WS adds "two additional categories" to WG's 19 when the actual form has 22 (it misses the outside-things/places-to-go split), and no published CDI version has 689 items. The published CDI:WS vocabulary checklist is 680 words in 22 semantic categories. 690 has no source anywhere: not in the report, not in the archive's companion documents, not in the literature.

**The source data.** The SPSS variable labels in `Combined groups.sav` name exactly the 22 American WS categories (sound effects … connecting words), and the `MacArthur2.xls` WS data-entry sheets carry exactly the American WS structure (22 vocabulary categories with no comprehension column, plus the Use/Suffix/irregulars/complexity sections; comprehension and signing were locally added extra questions, as the report says). Over the 154 WS rows, the maximum vocalised count attained equals the standard American category size _exactly_ in every category whose ceiling is reached: 43 children at exactly 12 sound effects, 7 at exactly 43 animals, 5 at exactly 103 action words, 14 at exactly 7 question words, 13 at exactly 12 time words, 11 at exactly 6 connecting words, and so on across 18 of the 22 categories. The remaining categories stay below their American sizes (food & drink max 65 of 68). The only values above an American size are isolated single rows: toys 20 and games 36 in one row (which also has no recorded age), question words 9 twice — one of the two in the row that also records pronouns as 55 of a possible 25 — and descriptive 66/69/77 as three lone rows above a clean pile-up at 61–63. A form with genuinely larger categories would show multiple children at the larger ceiling and a populated gap; instead every pile-up sits at the American size with a gap above it. These are entry errors, not a bigger checklist.

**The era's UK adaptations.** `CDIUSE.DOC` (same archive, Number project literature folder) is the centre's contemporaneous survey of UK CDI use: the UK versions circulating in the late 1990s (Atkinson's UCL/Cambridge adaptation, the Oxford modification, Bangor's variant) anglicised item wording; Atkinson's toddler adaptation reportedly produced "mini-norms that closely approximated the US norms". Word-for-word anglicisation leaves the counts unchanged. (The Oxford Babylab CDI held elsewhere in the archive is the instrument of the later 2011-era studies — uk_07's Reading CDI lineage — not uk_01's.)

**WG cross-check.** The report states WG = 396 (correct, American) and the WG data maxima respect it (max understood 326, max attained per category ≤ the WG sizes), so the report author transcribed the WG total correctly and mangled only the WS one. The WG side of `survey_vocab_max` was always right and is untouched.

The next full regeneration in `research-data-analysis` will reproduce it.

## Impact

No observed uk_01 WS count approaches the new ceiling: maximum spoken/produced is 669 (`produced` is the spoken + signed-only union, so it cannot double-count), so the form-ceiling guard drops nothing new. The near-ceiling implausible-production rule is scoped to ≤ 30 months, where the only two uk_01 WS rows have spoken ≤ 25 — no masking changes. `survey_vocab_max` is not the likelihood denominator for the Down syndrome models (counts are scored against the fixed 810-item DSE reference inventory, issue #128), so no fitted posterior is statistically wrong on account of the old value; the ceiling column rides in the prepared frame for the defect rules, the cross-lag audit and the trajectory overlays.

The prepared frame does hash the column's values, so the correction formally invalidates the fits of record of exactly the models whose frames carry uk_01 WS rows with `survey_vocab_max`. Measured by rebuilding every registered model's frame before and after the edit (`vocab_growth.analysis_frames`):

| Models                                                        | Frame hash | Fits of record      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------- |
| VG07, VG08, VG09, VG10, VG16, VG19, VG20, VG22                | changed    | invalidated — refit |
| VG01, VG02, VG05, VG11–VG15, VG21, VG23 (and TD-only VG03/04) | identical  | remain valid        |

Row counts are identical for every model, before and after. The raw-data fingerprint (which hashes every CSV in `data/`) changes for all fits, but since the same-day frame-hash rule (#272) a matching frame hash excuses a raw-data fingerprint mismatch — without it this correction would have staled every fit of record, including the twelve whose frames are untouched.

## Residual uncertainty

The blank uk_01 form itself (the report's Appendix II was a physical photocopy) has not been located in the digital archive, so 680 rests on the report's description of standard administration plus the category-ceiling evidence rather than on counting a surviving form. If a paper copy of the report or the questionnaire surfaces, counting Appendix II settles it beyond argument. The category asymmetry already documented in `uk_01_edg.md` (no comprehension column for places-to-go and helping verbs, and none for connecting words) is a property of how the local comprehension add-on was recorded, not of the checklist total.
