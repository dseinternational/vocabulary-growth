## The decision

**VG20 remains the model of record for the Down syndrome joint understood-plus-spoken estimands through the `us_03` ingestion refit. VG22 is promoted after that refit if and only if it meets the four criteria below, which are fixed now, before the evidence that would decide them exists.**

Three options were set out. **A**, keep VG20 and revisit, defers the question without setting its terms, so it would be argued again from the same evidence. **B**, promote VG22 now, buys the better description at the cost of re-deriving every child-level published number under a model with no recovery evidence and an unresolved rank, and would have to be revisited when the rank moves anyway. **C** was taken: decide once, conditionally, with the terms fixed in advance.

The discipline is the one [#288](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/288) used — criteria set before the data are seen — and it is taken here for the same reason. VG22 is the better description of these children on every measure that does not require validation, and the case against it is entirely that its distinguishing parameters have never been through the check that would establish they are identified. That is a question with an answer, and the answer is coming as part of work already scheduled.

## Criteria for promoting VG22

All four must be met, on the **refitted** pool with `us_03` in it.

1. **Recovery.** `fit_recovery.py vg22 --config rep`, three replicates, truth from the model of record's posterior, **all three assessed by the convergence gate**. `rho_uq`, both level scales (`tau_subj_u_0`, `tau_subj_q_0`) and both rate scales (`tau_subj_u_1`, `tau_subj_q_1`) inside their 89% intervals in at least two of three replicates, and no scale biased by more than 10%.
2. **Rank.** The rank re-selected on the refitted family, with rank 2 and rank 3 agreeing on the spoken child-rate scale to within about 1.5 combined standard errors. If they still disagree — they currently differ by about 2.8 — the disagreement is recorded as the reason not to promote, not worked around.
3. **Model comparison.** A **paired** `loo_compare` of VG20 against VG22 favouring VG22 by more than four standard errors, with the Pareto diagnostics reported beside it. The current 205-nat advantage is two independent totals differenced by hand, with no standard error, resting on 477 of 1,409 spoken rows whose Pareto shape exceeds 0.7.
4. **Out-of-sample.** The `us_03` scores repeated against the refitted models, with VG22 **no worse than VG20 on second-visit comprehension**. That is the one dimension where VG20 is currently better by a distinguishable margin (6.36 nats, z +2.89, on the comprehension-only conditioning).

Failing any of the four, VG20 keeps the role and the failing criterion is the recorded reason.

## Why this is not a close call in either direction

**What is not at stake.** The two models' population trajectories are interchangeable at every reported age — understood 218.8 against 219.5 at 36 months, spoken 35.6 against 35.8, matching between-study scales. No published population number moves either way. This is a child-level decision, exactly as the VG10 → VG20 decision was.

**What is at stake.** Three reported things read the child block: the Down syndrome / typically developing between-child spread contrast, the claim about which children convert comprehension into speech, and the child-level intervals. The level–level correlation is 0.311 [0.218, 0.400] under VG22 against 0.390 [0.314, 0.463] under VG20 — both now under the matched `eta = 2` prior, so the comparison is real and not a prior artefact. About a fifth of VG20's correlation is coupling that belongs to the rates.

**The case for VG22** is strong and mostly about description: +195 nats on spoken in sample, better out-of-sample production prediction, a between-child spread in the conversion _rate_ of 0.718 [0.620, 0.818] that VG20 cannot express, and the coupling that is the model's reason for existing — a child's comprehension **level** predicting their conversion **rate** at 0.393 [0.265, 0.518], stronger than `rho_uq` itself. It is also free of a specification fault VG20 has: giving VG20 the visit-1 production count _as well as_ comprehension makes its visit-2 comprehension prediction **worse** by 4.14 nats, concentrated in high-ratio children, where VG22 shows +0.03. More information making a prediction worse is a fault, not noise.

**The case against** is entirely validation. VG20 has `rep`-tier recovery with three of three replicates assessed and `rho_uq` inside its interval each time. VG22 has none at any tier, its rank is unresolved, and five of its six implied correlations still carry induced uniform priors rather than designed ones. This cycle supplied two fresh reasons to take that seriously: VG15's `test`-tier recovery **overstated** its own bias, and VG12's bias **survived** clean sampling when it might not have. Neither would have been known without running the check at reporting quality.

## Recorded regardless of the outcome

Two findings do not depend on which model wins and belong in the reported limitations now, not when the criteria are scored:

- **The child-effect correlation buys no out-of-sample predictive gain.** On 104 unseen children in `us_03`, on the task it exists to perform — predicting later production from earlier comprehension alone — VG20 is worse than all four comparators by about 1.8 nats, none of it distinguishable from zero.
- **Where a study's comprehension and conversion offsets point in opposite directions, VG20's correlation transfers a study effect into child-level predictions.** That is the mechanism behind the conditioning fault above, and `us_03` is a study with exactly that shape.

## Caveats on the criteria themselves

- They are scored on a pool `us_03` has changed. `us_03` contributes 104 children with two visits, which is precisely the design VG22's rates are identified from, so criterion 1 is being asked of a model in a more favourable position than it is in today. That is intentional — the question is whether the structure is supportable on the data we will have — but it means a pass is not evidence that VG22 was identified on the current pool.
- Criterion 3's four-standard-error threshold is a judgement, not a standard. It is set well above two because the comparison is between nested structures on shared data, where a difference favouring the richer model is expected before it is meaningful.
- Criterion 1's "no scale biased by more than 10%" is calibrated against what this cycle measured elsewhere: VG12's `tau_subject` is biased 5.3% and 8.0% and is treated as a real defect, so 10% is a permissive bar and a model failing it would be failing badly.
- Nothing here bears on VG19, which is a parallel refinement rather than a step in the same line and is not nested in VG22.
