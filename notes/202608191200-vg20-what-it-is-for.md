# What VG20 is for, and what building it taught us

> Written 2026-08-19, after VG20's promotion to model of record. The motivation was scattered across [202608151120](202608151120-vg16-cross-lag-quantified.md) §5, [202608151140](202608151140-cross-lag-not-for-models-of-record.md) §4 and [#224](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/224); the findings were in [202608190900](202608190900-vg20-promotion.md), which is a promotion record rather than an account of the model. This note is the account. Its last two sections answer a question neither of those addresses: **what does the correlated block do for separating overdispersion from real between-child heterogeneity?**

## 1. The one-sentence motivation

Every model from VG05 to VG16 gives each child two deviations — one on comprehension, one on the production ratio `q` — and draws them **independently**, which fixes the answer to "do children who understand more than expected also say a larger share of it?" at _no relationship_ before any data are seen. VG20 replaces that assumption with a free correlation, `rho_uq`, and lets the data answer.

Two earlier models had already left evidence that the assumption was wrong, both under priors pulling toward zero, so both lower bounds:

| Model    | Structure                            | Realised correlation of the fitted child deviations |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| VG10     | independent priors                   | +0.152 [0.105, 0.195]                               |
| VG16     | independent priors, cross-lag fitted | +0.134 [0.088, 0.180]                               |
| **VG20** | correlation estimated                | **+0.370** [0.305, 0.431]                           |

VG20's realised value reproduces its own fitted `rho_uq` of **+0.368** [0.287, 0.447]. The gap between +0.15 and +0.37 is the pull of a prior asserting zero, not a difference between the children — **VG10's own output contained the evidence against VG10's assumption**, and that is the cleanest single argument for the model.

## 2. What it changed, and what it did not

**Population level: nothing**, and that is required rather than reassuring — a correlation between two mean-zero deviations cannot move their mean. Gate 3 of #224 checked it at every reported age: 382 of 382 inside VG10's own 89% interval.

**Child level: this is the point.** Spoken vocabulary is `p_U · q`, so a positive correlation compounds — a child above average on comprehension also tends to convert more of it to speech. Subject-marginal intervals for words spoken are **9% wider at 90 months, rising to 33% at 12**. The gradient is explained: comprehension saturates, `p_U → 1`, and the spoken deviation approaches the `q` deviation alone. The correction is largest where the counts are smallest, which is where a family is most likely to be told what to expect.

**Downstream: the DS-versus-TD between-child contrast.** `comparison.child_spread_product` integrated over _independent_ deviations because no DS model estimated a correlation. It now takes the fitted `rho`, and τ_DS for spoken rises about **15% on the logit scale at every age** (1.473 → 1.694 at 8 months, 1.306 → 1.496 at 30), with comprehension unaffected. Every previously published spoken between-child contrast understated the Down syndrome side. The direction is conservative — DS was already the more variable side — and it repairs a reading the comparison book had said was not permitted, namely setting the spoken and comprehension ratios against each other.

**Sampling: no cost.** VG20 has 0 divergent transitions where VG10 has 1; energy is unchanged (BFMI 0.459–0.502 against 0.470–0.518). Adding a parameter usually costs something in geometry. This one did not, which is weak evidence that the independence constraint was a constraint the data were pushing against.

## 3. The channel that makes it work — and why it is _kappa_-free

This is the part that bears on overdispersion, and it is worth stating precisely because it is the reason [#229](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/229) option 3 exists.

Each administration yields **two** counts from **one** child on **one** day. The two Beta-Binomial draws are assumed independent across outcomes. So, linearising `log p_S = log p_U + log q`:

```
var(log p_U)               = tau_u²                    + [observation noise]
cov(log p_U, log p_S)      = tau_u² + rho·tau_u·tau_q  + [nothing]
```

**The covariance equation carries no observation-level noise term.** Everything that makes the two counts move together within a single administration is child-level, because the dispersion is independent across outcomes by construction. That is a second identifying equation for the between-child variance, available on **every administration carrying both outcomes** — 984 of 1,431 rows here — rather than only on the 335 children with a repeat visit. It is information about `tau` obtained without replication and without leaning on the Beta-Binomial's mean–variance functional form, which is the property option 3 wants for VG13.

### What forcing `rho = 0` actually costs — measured, after a wrong prediction

An earlier version of this section argued that setting `rho = 0` does not remove that equation but _over-constrains_ it to `cov = tau_u²`, so the covariance in the data must be absorbed elsewhere — and predicted `tau_subj_u`. **That prediction is wrong, and the way it is wrong is the interesting part.**

It was testable, so it was tested: simulate from VG20 with `rho_uq` pinned at the reported 0.368, then fit VG10 to the identical frame, with VG20 refitted to the same frame as the correctly specified control. Data and truth identical between arms, only the model differs, so any bias common to both — including the one in §5 — cancels. Three replicates ([`scripts/experiments/vg10_under_vg20_truth.py`](../scripts/experiments/vg10_under_vg20_truth.py)):

|                                  | VG10 − VG20, mean over replicates              |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `tau_subj_u`                     | **+1.05%**                                     |
| `tau_subj_q`                     | −0.06%                                         |
| `kappa_young_s`                  | +3.60%                                         |
| `kappa_old_s`                    | −2.99%                                         |
| largest of any reported quantity | 5.93% (`tau_q`, poorly recovered in both arms) |

**The covariance is not absorbed anywhere.** Not by `tau_subj_u`, not by the dispersion, not by anything reported. VG10's parameter estimates are very nearly VG20's on the same data.

What it cannot do is _express_ the correlation where the correlation matters. `rho` enters the child-level predictive directly — the subject-marginal draw takes both deviates from the joint distribution — so a model without it understates the compounding of `p_U` and `q` no matter how well its parameters are recovered:

| Age (mo)                   | 12            | 24        | 36        | 48        | 60        | 72        | 90        |
| -------------------------- | ------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- |
| VG10 / VG20 interval width | **0.74–0.79** | 0.75–0.80 | 0.79–0.85 | 0.86–0.89 | 0.88–0.91 | 0.89–0.92 | 0.91–0.93 |

Median 0.881 across all ages and replicates. Three independent lines agree: real data give 0.880 (gate 3 of #224, inverted), simulation at a known `rho` gives 0.881, and the analytic prediction from `var(log p_S) = var(log p_U) + var(log q) + 2·cov` gives `sqrt(2.269/3.012)` = 0.868 at young ages. The age gradient follows from the same equation — as comprehension saturates, `var(log p_U) → 0` takes the covariance term with it, so the ratio must approach 1, and it does.

**So VG10's defect is predictive, not estimative**, which also explains the paired LOO: +12.66 in VG20's favour on spoken and −0.30 on understood. An unmodelled covariance that no parameter absorbs has nowhere to go but the fit.

## 4. So does this disentangle overdispersion from true heterogeneity?

**Partly, and only for the joint models. It relocates the confound rather than removing it.**

What it does: supplies a `kappa`-free identifying equation for the child-level variance on 69% of rows instead of a replication-based one on 44% of children. That is real, and it is why the joint DS models are in better shape on this than the univariate TD ones.

What it does not do, in four parts:

**It cannot help VG11 or VG12.** They are single-outcome. There is no second count within an administration, so there is no cross-outcome channel at all. Those are the models [#225](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/225) is about, and the mechanism described here is unavailable to them by construction. Anyone reading VG20's success as progress on #225 has the wrong model family in mind.

**The kappa-free property depends on an assumption that shared reporting violates.** Both counts come from one questionnaire completed by one parent. A parent who over-reports over-reports both. That is an _occasion-level_ effect, and it enters `cov(log p_U, log p_S)` in exactly the place `rho·tau_u·tau_q` does. It is not separable within this design. So the covariance channel is free of _Beta-Binomial_ dispersion but not of _reporter_ dispersion, and the second is the one nobody has a handle on.

**That confound has a price tag.** VG16's cross-lag reads the child's earlier comprehension from a **different administration**, so it carries no shared-occasion term. Converted to the correlation scale it implies **+0.239** [0.110, 0.377] against VG20's **+0.368** [0.287, 0.447] — a factor of about **1.5**. The two overlap, and neither is a point estimate to publish unqualified, but together they **bracket**: VG16 reporter-free but attenuated and wide, VG20 sharp but reporter-inflated.

**And it does not touch the general problem.** The between-child scale comes back low in recovery on VG20 exactly as it does on VG10 and VG12 — the correlated block does not fix it. See §5, which is now a measurement rather than an open question.

## 5. The between-child scale comes back low, and it is not the sampler

This was queued in an earlier version as the first thing to check. It has been checked.

**Nine replicates, three models, all negative.** Comparing each fit's posterior median against the spread the simulated data _actually contain_ — the realised `tau × sd(raw deviates)`, not the nominal `tau`, because a refit can only recover what is in front of it:

| model | tier   | partition | bias vs realised spread | replicates negative |
| ----- | ------ | --------- | ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| VG10  | `test` | no        | **−6.25%** (sd 2.17)    | 3 of 3              |
| VG12  | `test` | **yes**   | **−5.94%** (sd 1.22)    | 3 of 3              |
| VG20  | `rep`  | no        | **−3.42%** (sd 2.43)    | 3 of 3              |

**It is not a `test`-tier artefact.** VG20 at `rep` converges cleanly on all three replicates (R-hat 1.002–1.004, ESS 1,642–3,283, against 1 of 3 converging at `test`), and the point estimates move by at most 0.0015 — 0.19% of the truth. Quadrupling the chains changed nothing.

**It is not the prior.** `tau_subj_u ~ HalfNormal(1.5)` has prior mean 1.197, above the truths of 0.78–0.81, so prior shrinkage pulls _up_. The same argument holds on VG12, where [#225](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/225) makes it about the `share` prior.

**It is not the partition.** Present at similar size with and without.

**And the magnitude is less certain than a single figure suggests.** The realised spread of 767 standard-normal deviates has a sampling SE of 2.55%, so each replicate's comparator wobbles by that much before the model does anything; the earlier "−5.8%" quoted to two significant figures was over-precise. What nine replicates support is a **downward bias of roughly 3–6%, in every model tried, present at both sampling tiers and with or without the partition** — not a point estimate.

That is the fact #229's options have to be ranked against, and it is not the fact the issue was opened on.

## 6. What to check next, in order

The two checks this note originally queued here — `rep`-tier recovery, and simulating from VG20 to fit VG10 — have both been run; §5 and §3 are their results. What remains:

1. **VG12 at `rep`.** The only arm not yet run at full length (~10.3 h; its own `rep` fit is 206 minutes and a recovery replicate costs about the same). VG20's `rep` arm has already shown the bias is not a tier artefact, so this would confirm the _partitioned_ model behaves the same way rather than decide anything. Deferred on that basis, 2026-08-19.

2. **Find the mechanism of the −3 to −6%.** It is not the tier, not the prior's location, and not the partition, and no option in [#229](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/229) currently addresses it — the issue was opened believing the partition was the cause. Candidates worth separating: the boundary at zero and the resulting skew in a weakly-identified scale's posterior; shrinkage geometry in a design where most children appear once; and whether the bias scales with the number of singleton children, which is testable by simulating pools with the replication structure varied and nothing else.

3. **Do not extend the correlated block to VG15 on VG20's authority.** VG15 carries `use_subject_re_u`, `use_subject_re_q` _and_ `use_subject_re_sign`, all independent — the same assumption on three effects. But its realised deviations correlate at only **+0.064** [+0.013, +0.114] on the understood × `q` pair, against VG10's +0.152, and the two sign pairs span zero. Its `q` is defined against the sign/speech composition and its frame differs, so the two are not strictly comparable — but the evidence is materially thinner and VG15 needs its own case.

4. **VG13 under #229 option 3** is the live TD candidate, and §4's second and third points are the reason it is not a free win: a TD pool has _more_ singleton administrations than the DS pool, not fewer, so the shared-reporter inflation there would be larger than the 1.5× measured here.
