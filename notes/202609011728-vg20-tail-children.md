# VG20's heavy-tailed child effects: naming the children in the tails

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted by an LLM-based AI tool (Claude Code/Fable 5).

**2026-09-01.** A tail diagnostic on the VG20 fit of record earlier today ([202609011717](202609011717-multivariate-t-not-adopted.md), whose harness is promoted to `scripts/experiments/vg20_child_effect_tails.py`) found the standardised child random effects mildly heavy-tailed: per-draw cross-child excess kurtosis +0.47 on the comprehension margin (`u`) and +0.34 on the production-ratio margin (`q`) against a normal reference band of roughly ±0.27 for 767 children, with on average 4.6 (`u`) and 3.4 (`q`) children beyond 3 SD per draw against a normal expectation of 2.1 — and `rho_uq` robust to trimming (top-1% Mahalanobis children move it by −0.003). This note answers the follow-up question: **which** children sit in the tails, and are they a data-defect signature the masking rules do not yet catch, or real developmental heterogeneity? The answer is: both, in identifiable parts — about a third of the combined tail excess is defect-linked (28% of the `u` margin's, 45% of the `q` margin's) and the rest is corroborated heterogeneity — and the split is informative. No masking rule or model was changed; this is a findings record.

## Method, and why the identification had to come from the trace

The fit of record (`VG20-age-understood-spoken-ds-re-subj-uq-anchored-corr`, created 2026-08-22, 1,431 rows, 767 children across the 14 Down syndrome studies) predates the 2026-08-25 comprehension-below-production rule and the 2026-08-31 uk_01 identifier fixes, ie_02 withholding and us_01 same-day rule, so today's rebuilt analysis frame no longer matches it (1,424 rows, 763 children; the frame-hash mismatch is the known by-design staleness recorded in [202608311830](202608311830-steep-within-child-gains.md)). Mapping trace subject indices through today's frame would therefore mis-attribute every child after the first removed key. Instead the fit-time observation table was reconstructed from the trace's own `constant_data` and `observed_data` groups (ages are stored raw, in months; study codes decode against the sorted study list in the manifest), and each fit-time child was matched to a current `subject_key` by their study + age + count signature. 739 of 767 matched exactly (two of them to a pair of current uk_01 children whose single recorded rows are identical — indistinguishable, and nowhere near the tails); the 28 signature changes decompose precisely into the post-fit rules' footprint (the ten comprehension masks, the withheld ie_02 administration, the two us_01 same-day masks, the withheld uk_01 fused child, and six uk_01 single-visit records re-linked pairwise into three longitudinal children, the re-linked unions verified value-for-value) plus eight children — five uk_01, three uk_02 — whose same-age dual-administration rows changed only their tie order under the deterministic row sort introduced after the fit, verified by multiset comparison to carry identical data: a matching artefact, not a data change.

Per child and margin, the standardised effect `e = delta_subj_* / tau_subj_*` was computed over 18,000 draws (6 chains × 6,000, thinned 2×), summarised as the posterior mean and the posterior probability `P(|e| > 3)`, plus a per-draw Mahalanobis distance using `rho_uq`. Since the per-draw expected count of children beyond 3 SD is the sum of these probabilities over children, the probabilities attribute the tail excess exactly.

## Group A — defects the current rules already resolve (fit predates them)

Two of the five `u`-tail children are **ie_01 `ID_674B16BE4C940DC3` and `ID_6E2E02A457C9257A`** (posterior mean `e_u` −2.84 and −2.79; `P(|e_u|>3)` 0.38 and 0.34): the two "0 understood against 83 spoken" administrations named in the comprehension-below-production rule's docstring. The fit was made three days before that rule landed, so their zero comprehension counts were still in the likelihood. Together they carry ~0.72 of the 4.62 per-draw `u` exceedance; a refit of record removes that much of the tail for free.

A residual on the same two children is worth recording, report-only. Both are ie_01 _follow-up_ administrations whose comprehension section is blank-scored-as-zero and whose production is partially completed: their retained `spoken = 83` counts are contradicted by the same children's baseline says totals (346 at 66 months and 321 at 58 months — vocabulary does not shrink by three quarters in six months). The contradiction is invisible to the current rules because the baseline rows never reach the frame (the baseline wave is excluded as a partial administration) and the longitudinal-collapse rule is deliberately age-scoped to ≤ 30 months. So even after the comprehension masks, these two children will re-enter a refit as extreme low-production observations on counts that look like incomplete administrations rather than measurements.

## Group B — sub-threshold defect signatures the rules do not catch (both in the `q` high tail)

**ie_02 `ID_54CAADEF3C0D8425`** — the `q` margin's single largest contributor (`e_q` +2.84, `P(|e_q|>3)` 0.41). At t1 (16 months) the raw row reads understood 39, imitates 38, spoken 37, says-clearly 32 — a near-identical cascade across the response columns, which is the duplicated-outcome signature (spoken/understood = 0.95 in infancy) below that rule's `understood ≥ 100` floor. At t2 (19 months) spoken falls 37 → 0 — the longitudinal-collapse signature below its `≥ 50` floor. One administration, two sub-threshold signatures; the same internal-contradiction class as the withheld `ID_62C63BE2B3B627E6` t2 ([202608311830](202608311830-steep-within-child-gains.md)), at smaller counts. Which wave is trustworthy cannot be recovered from the aggregates.

**uk_05 `ID_A3AB8793F223300C`** — `e_q` +2.70, `P(|e_q|>3)` 0.20, and the second-highest joint Mahalanobis in the cohort. At 25 months understood, spoken and signed are all exactly 66; at 30 months spoken and signed are both exactly 82 against understood 95. Across all 46 uk_05 rows carrying both production columns, this child's two visits are the **only** rows where spoken equals signed above zero, and the 25-month visit is the only row where all three columns coincide — while the same child's 18-month visit (15/1/12) shows ordinary separation. That is the shape of a column written across the others, not of a child who signs every spoken word.

These two are the whole of the uncaught-defect class, not the visible part of a cluster: a pool-wide scan for the sub-threshold duplicated-outcome signature (spoken ≥ 0.75 × understood, 20 ≤ understood < 100, age ≤ 30 months) finds only these two children plus one borderline us_01 same-day row whose child is not in the tails (`e_q` +2.0), and a scan for sub-floor collapses within the rule's age window (spoken 20–49 at ≤ 30 months falling ≥ 5× by a later visit) finds only the ie_02 child.

## Group C — plausibly real developmental heterogeneity (the rest, and most of the `q` low tail)

The `q` low tail is a recognisable minimally-verbal subgroup, and its members are corroborated rather than contradicted by the columns the model does not see:

- **uk_01 `ID_FC82A93593817536`** (`e_q` −2.69, `P` 0.35): spoken 0 at 81 months — but signed 335. A prolific signer whose entire production is in the modality VG20's spoken outcome cannot see.
- **uk_06 `ID_E9EF80F9C04D6F24`** (`e_q` −2.54): understood 676, spoken 18, signed 26 at 115 months — internally consistent minimal speech at large comprehension.
- **it_01 `ID_1F3511A1B5734D26`** (`e_q` −2.60): five visits from 37 to 61 months, comprehension growing 118 → 325 while spoken goes 0, 0, 1, 3, 20 — the classic profile, longitudinally coherent.
- **ie_01 `ID_021BBD75CF6530BD`** and **`ID_A0793D1D79320511`** (`e_q` −2.46, −2.41): says 0 at 59 and 70 months on comprehension of 198 and ~32, both consistent between baseline and follow-up waves, both recorded as hearing-impaired with aids.
- **uk_01 `ID_58F30389FDB40F6E`** (`e_q` −2.50): understood 179, produced 22 (18 spoken + 4 signed) at 78 months — minimally verbal in both modalities.
- **us_02 `ID_602C4770FCFF9728`** (`e_q` −2.37): understood 32, spoken 0 at 65 months; no corroborating columns in this source, but the same profile.

The tenth flagged `q` child sits on the **high** side and is also plausibly real: **ie_02 `ID_D8967F99A1CD0D04`** (`e_q` +2.70, `P` 0.17) has understood 114 → 219, spoken 68 → 128 and signed 74 → 144 across visits at 29 and 32 months — every column growing coherently between waves, a genuinely strong producer and signer rather than a column artefact.

The remaining `u`-tail children are corroborated in the other direction. **ie_02 `ID_67F18AD5513389FD`** — the single largest tail contributor anywhere (`e_u` −3.64, `P(|e_u|>3)` 0.89) — has all five vocabulary columns at zero at both 32 and 35 months, and the study's adaptive-behaviour columns independently record the lowest scores of all 58 scored ie_02 administrations (composite 42, communication 24, against study medians of about 78 on both). **ie_02 `ID_5228EDE8DD89B881`** (`e_u` −3.05) understands 0 then 1 word at 23 and 26 months with adaptive scores in the study's bottom sixth (69/58). At the top, **ie_02 `ID_08D9517D8BDE1360`** (`e_u` +3.07, `P` 0.61) understands 244 then 360 words at 15 and 18 months with spoken 2 and 1 — the pool maximum under 20 months, but the extreme of a _multi-study_ right tail (es_01 records 321, 267 and 251 at 15–20 months; two further ie_02 children sit at 228; us_01's retained sensitivity-target records at 213–217) rather than an isolated value, with adaptive scores in the study's top decile (89/96) and internally consistent growth. High parent-report comprehension in infancy may well be measurement leniency, but it is a shared property of the instrument across studies, not a defect signature of this record. The three ie_01 high performers in the mild `u` tail (659–776 understood at 46–69 months, `P` ≈ 0.09–0.13) are the family the implausible-production rule's age scope deliberately leaves alone — its docstring's apparently-legitimate example, the child with 741 of 810 spoken at 69 months, is one of the three, and the other two never matched any signature at all — and each is consistent with its own baseline wave.

## What this means for VG20

The mild heavy tails are not a diffuse model deficiency and not a threat to the headline estimates: it is about a dozen nameable children, the correlation `rho_uq` is already known to be insensitive to them, and just over a quarter of the `u` margin's excess (Group A) disappears at the next refit of record under rules that already exist. What remains splits cleanly. The `q` low tail is _substantive_ — a minimally-verbal subgroup that is real, longitudinally coherent, and in one flagged case signing-dominant (335 signed words against zero spoken), meaning VG20's spoken-only production outcome understates that child's expressive vocabulary by construction — and uk_07's `ID_9C77404E804EA436`, just below the flagging cut (`P(|e_q|>3)` = 0.08), has the same shape, understanding 100 → 170 words at 81 and 95 months while producing 22 then 49 words mostly in sign. The trivariate models (VG14/VG15) see the signed modality and are the right instrument for these children. The `q` high tail is where the two genuine uncaught-defect candidates live, both with production counts propped up by administrations whose columns appear to have bled into each other below the existing rules' floors.

Nothing here recommends widening the rules by lowering their thresholds: the sub-threshold scans show the floors are excluding almost nothing else, so a general threshold change would trade two records for new false positives elsewhere (the duplicated-outcome docstring's own analysis of legitimate high ratios at older ages). If the two Group B administrations are to be masked or withheld, that is a per-record ruling for the study owners, on the same evidence-and-provenance footing as `IE02_WITHHELD_ADMINISTRATIONS` — and worth making before the next reporting-quality refit round, since the frame change would otherwise stale the refits a second time. A Student-t child effect was measured and not adopted the same day ([202609011717](202609011717-multivariate-t-not-adopted.md)) on exactly the reasoning this identification vindicates: a t silently downweights children who are either nameable defects for the rules or a real subgroup, and on these numbers the residual tail after the Group A and B rulings is a handful of corroborated real children — the minimally-verbal subgroup and the adaptive-score-backed comprehension extremes — which the Gaussian's mild under-coverage already flags honestly.
