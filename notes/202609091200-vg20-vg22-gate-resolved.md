## The outcome

**VG20 remains the model of record for the Down syndrome joint understood-plus-spoken estimands. Criterion 2, the rank, is the recorded reason.**

The 2026-09-03 decision fixed four criteria before the evidence existed and made the rule explicit: _"Failing any of the four, VG20 keeps the role and the failing criterion is the recorded reason."_ Criterion 2 fails, and it fails by more than it did before the refit.

## Criterion 2, measured

The criterion asks for the rank to be re-selected on the refitted family, "with rank 2 and rank 3 agreeing on the spoken child-rate scale to within about 1.5 combined standard errors". That scale is `tau_subj_q_1`.

**4.06 combined standard errors against a threshold of about 1.5.** The decision note's instruction for this outcome is unambiguous: _"If they still disagree — they currently differ by about 2.8 — the disagreement is recorded as the reason not to promote, not worked around."_

**The disagreement widened rather than closing.** It was 2.85 SEs before the `us_03` ingestion. The expectation going in was that it might close, because `us_03` contributed 104 children with two visits and repeat visits are what identify a child _rate_ at all. Both scales did rise on the larger pool — rank 2 from 0.348 to 0.407, rank 3 from 0.576 to 0.730 — but rank 3 rose more than twice as far, so the gap grew.

**Everything else reproduces [`202608231420`](202608231420-vg22-factor-anchor-bimodality.md) §5's reading**, which is what makes this a clean result rather than a noisy one. The intercept-level quantities are indistinguishable across the two ranks — `rho_uq` 0.11 SEs apart, `tau_subj_u_0` 0.05, `tau_subj_q_0` 0.58 — and it is the child _slope_ alone that separates them. A default rank that nearly halves a reported child-rate scale relative to the defensible alternative is making a substantive claim, and the residual likelihood cannot adjudicate it.

**Rank 2 is also the worse-behaved fit**, as it was before the anchor-order fix: 154 divergent transitions against rank 3's 2, at the same target-accept. Both clear R-hat and ESS; both fail the four-check gate on divergences alone.

### Provenance

The two fits differ in the rank and in nothing else, which is what licenses reading the difference as the rank's. Both are `rep` (6 chains × 6,000 draws after 6,000 tuning, target-accept 0.95, seed 47), on **the same prepared frame** — hash `sha256:b27f64ea0640…`, 1,708 rows, 943 children — and the same executable-code signature `114e70cf…`, both with clean trees. Their manifests' `subject_factor` payloads differ in one field, `rank` 3 against 2, with `ref_age_months`, `rho_uq_eta` and both `tau1` prior scales held equal; the traces carry six loading elements against four. The rank-2 fit took 1 h 09 m.

## Criteria 3 and 4: restated, run, and not scorable at `test` tier

Both were restated because their original statistics were invalidated (the status note sets out why); the study owner accepted criterion 3's substitute and criterion 4's on 2026-09-09. Both were then run. **Neither is scorable at the tier they were run at**, and neither can change the outcome, but what they show belongs on the record.

| run                                           | folds failing the gate | worst R-hat | worst ESS |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------: | ----------: | --------: |
| Criterion 3 — whole-subject holdout           |                9 of 10 |      1.0255 |       265 |
| Criterion 4 — later-waves, comprehension-only |                8 of 10 |      1.0249 |       213 |

Against hard gates of R-hat ≤ 1.01 and ESS ≥ 400. `kfold_loso.py` flags both comparisons `all_folds_converged=False`, and its own doctrine is that such an elpd _"must not be read as a model comparison"_. This is a sampling shortfall, not a design fault: `test` is 4 chains × 2,000 draws and the gate is applied element-wise over every free variable, including `subject_factor_z`'s 943 × k per-child elements. VG22's own `rep` fit has a minimum ESS of 2,678; at 22% of the draws one would predict about 590, and 213–545 is what appeared.

**What they say, which must not be read as a comparison:**

| comparison                                       | VG22 − VG20 | paired SE |      SEs |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------: | --------: | -------: |
| Criterion 3, whole-subject holdout, 943 children |      +65.56 |     19.78 |     3.31 |
| Criterion 4, later waves, both outcomes          |      +33.61 |      9.85 |     3.41 |
| **Criterion 4, later-wave comprehension alone**  |   **+0.63** |  **2.05** | **0.31** |

Two things are worth recording even so, because they bear on any later attempt.

**Criterion 3 would not have rescued the promotion in any case.** Its threshold is four standard errors. Even taken at face value — which it must not be — 3.31 falls short of its own bar.

**Criterion 4's specific concern appears to have dissolved.** It exists because VG20 was better than VG22 at predicting second-visit comprehension by a distinguishable margin, 6.36 nats at z +2.89, and that was the one dimension where VG20 led. On 425 repeat-measured children under the restated holdout the two models are **indistinguishable** on that quantity: 0.63 nats, 0.31 standard errors, in VG22's direction. If that survives a converged run, the fault criterion 4 was built to police is no longer present — which sharpens rather than softens the finding that the _rank_ is what stands in VG22's way.

**VG22's fold fits are consistently the worse behaved** across both runs: 27 divergences against VG20's 1 under whole-subject holdout, 5 against 0 under later-waves, and worse R-hat in most folds. That is consistent with the case against VG22 being about identification rather than about fit.

**Cost to make them scorable**, if that is ever wanted: the ten criterion-3 fold fits totalled 1 h 31 m at `test`. `rep-lite` (4 × 4,000/4,000, target-accept 0.95) doubles the draws, should roughly double the minimum ESS to about 530, and raises target-accept — call it three hours. Full `rep` is about seven. Neither can change this decision.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled.** VG20 keeps the Down syndrome joint role. On [#320](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/320), VG22 is not promoted and stays `unclassified` pending its own role decision; VG19, whose retirement case leaned on VG22's promotion, must now be decided on its own evidence, as [`202609031930`](202609031930-vg20-vg22-decision.md) always said it should be (_"nothing here bears on VG19 … not nested in VG22"_).

**Not settled.** Whether VG22 is the better description of these children is not answered here and is not what the gate asked. VG22 remains, on the evidence in [`202609031415`](202609031415-vg20-vs-vg22.md), the richer structure; what the gate establishes is that its rank cannot be selected on the data, and that a scale which halves between two defensible ranks is not one to publish. The route to reopening it is a rank that the evidence can choose — not more comparisons at the current one.
