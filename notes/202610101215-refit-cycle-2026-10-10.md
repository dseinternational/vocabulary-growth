# Refit cycle 2026-10-10: four DS models on the workstation, the rest on a VM

> [!NOTE]
> Drafted with assistance from Claude Code/Opus 5.5.

**Date:** 2026-10-10. **Tag:** `fits/2026-10-10` at `2513c27` (#393); CI passed on that commit. **Replaces:** the `fits/2026-10-02` campaign, which was planned for a VM and never launched. **Decided by the study owner today:** fit the four Down syndrome publication-scope models, VG15, VG20, VG24 and VG25, at `rep` on the Apple Silicon workstation as this cycle's fits of record, then run everything else, including every typically developing model, on a dedicated VM from the same tag.

## Why a new tag

Eleven pull requests merged after `fits/2026-10-02`. Several change the executable-code signature: the shared likelihood builders and posterior extraction (#384), the public diagnostic and permission helpers (#386, #387), the design-token figure colours (#390) and `dse-research-utils` 0.16.2, 0.17.0 and 0.18.0 (#383, #385, #390, #391). Resume, strict sync and publication would refuse a fit made at the old tag against `main`, and the old tag would reproduce code that `main` has replaced. Every existing fit is stale under the new signature, so the cycle refits the whole registry.

## Part 1: the workstation fits

The host is an Apple M3 Max with 16 cores (12 performance, 4 efficiency) and 48 GB of memory, running macOS 27. The fits ran from a worktree at `../vocabulary-growth-fits`, detached at the tag, with its own `uv sync --locked` environment and prepared data. The output root is `~/output/vocabulary-growth-fits-2026-10-10`, outside both checkouts. The worktree stayed clean throughout.

The command is stage A's `A-fit-ds` step at two fits wide instead of four, run in a detached `screen` session under `caffeinate -ims` so that the machine does not sleep:

```bash
PYTENSOR_FLAGS=cxx=$HOME/.local/share/pytensor-cxx/clang++ \
  pwsh -NoProfile -File scripts/run_replication.ps1 -Config rep \
  -OutputDir ~/output/vocabulary-growth-fits-2026-10-10 -ReplaceModelOfRecord \
  -RenderOnFit -NoCompare -NoRender -NoUpload -MaxParallel 2 -MinFreeGB 4 \
  -Models vg25,vg24,vg15,vg20
```

Two fits at six chains each occupy the twelve performance cores. The models are ordered longest first so that the two slots finish close together.

### Two incidents before the first sample

**PyTensor's `-ld64` flag.** The first launch failed VG25 and VG24 within 30 seconds with `ld: library 'd64' not found`. PyTensor 3.3.3 appends `-ld64` to every C compilation on macOS 15 and later, to select Apple's classic linker. The macOS 27 Xcode tools (Apple clang 21, `ld-27037`) no longer recognise the flag and read it as a library name. The problem is open upstream as [pytensor#2268](https://github.com/pymc-devs/pytensor/issues/2268), with a fix proposed in [pytensor#2432](https://github.com/pymc-devs/pytensor/pull/2432), and PyTensor's `main` still adds the flag. A newer PyTensor would change the lock and the signature, so it cannot enter this cycle. The relaunch sets `PYTENSOR_FLAGS=cxx=` to a wrapper outside the repository that drops the one flag and uses the default linker. The wrapper does not touch the package source or library versions, so the signature is unchanged. The [environment guide](../docs/runbooks/environment-locks.md#pytensors-linker-flag-on-macos-27) now describes the wrapper. The two failed attempts are retained under `failed/` in the output root, with the driver output in `launch-attempt1-ld64.out`. Linux VMs are unaffected.

**The driver's memory floor on Apple Silicon.** The driver held VG24 back on the first launch, reading 5.8 GB available while `memory_pressure` reported 82% free. `Get-AvailableMemoryGB` in `scripts/run_replication.ps1` multiplied `vm_stat` page counts by 4,096 bytes, but Apple Silicon pages are 16,384 bytes, so the driver under-read available memory by a factor of four. At the default 16 GB floor, a second fit would never have launched beside the first and the run would have been silently serial. The relaunch passes `-MinFreeGB 4`, which is about 16 GB of real memory under the bug. `main` now reads the page size from `vm_stat`'s header. The pinned worktree keeps the old script, because editing it would mark the fits dirty. Linux reads `MemAvailable` and is unaffected.

### Results

The relaunched run took 43 minutes, from 11:11 to 11:54 UTC, and every step succeeded. All four fits passed every convergence check at the first attempt, with no escalation, divergence or caveat:

Wall time is the driver's, including the report render. Peak memory is the largest resident size sampled every 30 seconds, so a shorter peak may have been missed. The output root holds 19 GB in total. Every manifest records commit `2513c27`, a clean checkout, the `rep` configuration and the `numba` backend on `macOS-27.0.1-arm64`.

From the worktree, `check_fit.py vg15 vg20 vg24 vg25 --config rep` reports all four `[valid]` for `--purpose resume` and for `--purpose publish`, the strict purpose without the caveat allowance. Each model report rendered without an error cell.

Two things in the reports are for the next cycle, not this one. The trace plot crowds up to 40 parameters onto one fixed-size figure (`common.py`, `az.plot_trace` at `FIGSIZE_XL`), so titles overlap the tick labels and matplotlib warns that constrained layout collapsed. The chains themselves are legible and stationary. Changing it would change the executable signature, so it waits. Each fit's leave-one-out step also reports Pareto k above 0.7 for some observations. Every fit drops 12 words-spoken rows with a constant log-likelihood from that step, and VG15 and VG24 also drop six words-signed rows. These are the known limits of PSIS-LOO with child effects, not new findings.

## Part 2: the VM campaign

The VM runs `scripts/vm/campaign.sh` from a clone at `fits/2026-10-10`, following the [VM runbook](../docs/runbooks/vm-refit.md) with one addition: the workstation's output root is copied into the VM's output root before the first stage. The executable-code signature records source hashes and package versions, not the platform. Resume validation compares the definition, the prepared frame, the sampling effort, the signature, the commit and a clean checkout, and reads every file relative to the fit directory. The four workstation fits should therefore validate on the VM, and stage A's `A-fit-ds` step should skip them as complete and compatible. If any does not validate, that step refits it, so the campaign is correct either way. Only the time differs.

1. **Provision** as the runbook's section 1 describes: at least 32 vCPUs, at least 128 GB of memory, x86_64 preferred, a 128 GB swap backstop, user lingering and the report fonts. The bootstrap script at this tag checks for `pnpm` and fails without it. Install pnpm before bootstrapping, as described in the [environment guide](../docs/runbooks/environment-locks.md#install-the-documentation-tools). Install AzCopy as well as the Azure CLI for the copies in steps 2 and 4.
2. **Archive the workstation root to internal storage.** Copy the whole of `~/output/vocabulary-growth-fits-2026-10-10` to the internal trace archive, storage account `dsestdsresswc`, container `outputs`, prefix `vocabulary-growth/2513c27/`. Never use the public container: traces contain observation-level data and identifiers.
3. **Bootstrap** with `bash bootstrap.sh fits/2026-10-10 --output-root /data/vocabulary-growth --with-tests` and fix whatever it reports until it ends with `Ready`.
4. **Seed the VM's output root** from the archive into `/data/vocabulary-growth`. Then confirm the four fits from the clone with `uv run python scripts/check_fit.py vg15 vg20 vg24 vg25 --config rep --purpose resume`. Record the result. An `[invalid]` line names what differs. The cause is then most likely a package that resolved differently on Linux, and step 5 refits the model.
5. **Plan and launch.** Run `scripts/vm/campaign.sh plan A B C D P`, then `scripts/vm/campaign.sh launch A B C D P`. Stage A's DS step validates the seeded fits; its TD step fits VG11, VG12, VG21, VG23 and VG26 one at a time. Stage B fits the eleven DS development steps in a pool and then VG03, VG04 and VG13 alone.
6. **Escalate** the known cases as the runbook's section 5 describes: VG09 high tuning, `vg15 dse-native-only`, `vg15 fallback-dispersion` on aarch64 and VG11's recorded exception. Record each escalation and every step marked done by hand.
7. **Stage P** regenerates the comparisons and robustness matrices, syncs the figure cache with `--allow-caveats` and renders the report book for validation. Review the recorded caveats, then put publication of the model reports and the comparison book to the study owner. The report book is not uploaded.
8. **Bring the root home and archive it**, the whole root and not only `models/`, under the same `vocabulary-growth/2513c27/` prefix. Verify file counts and sizes per directory before deallocating the VM.

### Expected cost

The [VM runbook](../docs/runbooks/vm-refit.md#what-a-campaign-runs) estimates stage A at 15 hours, B at 10 hours, C at 2.5 to 3 days, D at 9 to 10 days and P at 2 to 3 hours, from the September workstation cycles. Seeding the four DS fits removes stage A's DS pool, which leaves the five TD fits. Four of them took 8 hours 14 minutes on the workstation in September (VG11 3 h 59 m, VG12 54 m, VG21 1 h 45 m, VG23 1 h 36 m). VG26 has no `rep` timing yet. The campaign is still about two weeks of machine time, dominated by stage D's typically developing arms and held-out folds.

The campaign fits 23 registered models, 118 sensitivity arms (42 in stage C and 76 in stage D) and 18 recovery cells of three replicates each, before the Gompertz comparison and the held-out folds. At the September means of 6.6 GB per reporting model, 3.8 GB per sensitivity fit and 5.0 GB per recovery fit, that is roughly 150 + 450 + 270 GB at the `full` trace tier, before the TD arms' larger traces and promotion copies. The bootstrap check asks for 1 TB free. A 2 TB managed disk leaves room for the typically developing outliers, such as VG11's 24.6 GB trace, and for the staged and previous copies made during promotion.

### What the split does not change

Validation treats a fit from either host alike, so the run record must say where each fit was made. The manifest's runtime section records the platform. If `main` moves during the campaign, only commits that change nothing hashed or validated can be brought into the VM clone, as the [full-refit runbook](../docs/runbooks/full-refit.md#advancing-the-worktree) describes. The workstation worktree stays at the tag until the set is superseded.
