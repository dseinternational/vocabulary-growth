## Headline

**#226 softens, #225 hardens, and the mechanism #225 proposes does not survive contact with VG11.** VG15's sign–speech association is still biased low but by roughly half what the `test` tier suggested, with the truth inside its interval in two of three replicates rather than outside in all three. VG12's between-child scale is still biased low under clean sampling, and the z-scores get _worse_ rather than better. VG11's between-child scale is biased **high**, through a different route, which means the variance partition alone does not explain either.

## VG15 at `rep`: the escalation was needed, and #226 overstates the bias

All three replicates cleared the gate — max R-hat 1.0097 / 1.0081 / 1.0068, minimum ESS 1,029–1,279, and no divergence caveats — against **none of three** at `test`, where R-hat ran 1.015 to 1.020. VG15 needing `rep` for recovery is now established the same way VG20's was.

The quantity #226 is about:

| Replicate | `psi` truth | Posterior mean | z     | Truth inside 89%? | `psi_study` inside, of 14 |
| --------- | ----------- | -------------- | ----- | ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| r01       | 2.354       | 1.980          | -1.54 | yes               | 14                        |
| r02       | 2.309       | 1.855          | -1.87 | no                | 3                         |
| r03       | 2.442       | 2.226          | -0.78 | yes               | 14                        |

**The direction survives; the severity does not.** `psi` comes back low in three of three, which is itself evidence — under correct calibration the sign is a coin flip and three of three has probability 0.125 — but the magnitude is roughly half what #226 reports from the `test` tier, where the headline replicate read truth 2.549 against a posterior of 1.917 at z = -2.65 with the truth outside the interval every time. At `rep` the truth is inside the 89% interval in two of three and the largest z is -1.87.

`tau_psi` recovers without a consistent sign (-1.47, -0.05, +0.59) and is inside its interval in all three, so the "underestimated `tau_psi` over four informing sources" part of #226 is not reproduced here at all.

The severe over-shrinkage of the per-study `psi_study` values is real in exactly one replicate — 3 of 14 inside in r02, against 14 of 14 in both others. That is now an occasional failure rather than the characteristic behaviour #226 describes.

**#226's conclusion still stands in the direction that matters for the report**: the reported `psi` of 2.34 is more likely conservative than inflated, and the per-study spread is the part to distrust. Its stated magnitudes should be replaced with these.

VG15's overall calibration is the best of the three studied: interval coverage 0.927 / 0.778 / 0.944 over 248 targets each, pooling to 0.883 against a nominal 0.89. The per-child aggregates are close to nominal too — `delta_subj_u` 0.896 and `delta_subj_q` 0.904 over 763 children — while `delta_subj_sign` is lower at 0.835 with a truth-to-posterior correlation of only 0.33, which is the signed modality being weakly identified per child rather than a calibration failure.

## VG12 at `rep`: #225 confirmed, and clean sampling makes it worse

| Replicate | Max R-hat | Min ESS | `tau_subject` truth | Posterior | z         | Inside 89%? | Bias      |
| --------- | --------- | ------- | ------------------- | --------- | --------- | ----------- | --------- |
| r01       | 1.0030    | 2,600   | 0.662               | 0.627     | **-2.99** | no          | **-5.3%** |
| r02       | 1.0018    | 3,095   | 0.708               | 0.651     | **-4.70** | no          | **-8.0%** |

Against the `test` tier's R-hat of 1.006 and 1.011 and ESS near 580, this is a large improvement in sampling and **no improvement at all in recovery**: the bias is the same size and the z-scores are larger, because the posteriors are tighter around the same wrong place. That is the cleanest available demonstration that #225 is an identification problem rather than a mixing artefact.

The decomposition #225 proposes reproduces exactly. The variance budget recovers cleanly and the split does not:

| Quantity                 | r01 z | r02 z | Inside 89%? |
| ------------------------ | ----- | ----- | ----------- |
| `v_total`                | +0.45 | +0.04 | yes, both   |
| `subject_variance_share` | -2.20 | -2.96 | no, both    |
| `tau_subject`            | -2.99 | -4.70 | no, both    |

Since `tau_subject` is the square root of the product, an under-recovered share becomes an under-recovered scale, amplified.

**One quantity is newly implicated and is not in #225**: the between-**study** scale `tau` recovers 52% and 59% high (z +0.80, +0.86, inside its interval both times because it is poorly determined). The study scale being biased upward while the child scale is biased downward is consistent with variance migrating between the two levels, which is a sharper statement of the identification problem than the partition alone.

Both `rep` replicates still carry divergences (14 and 1) and energy BFMI below 0.3 (0.299, 0.276), so VG12's geometry is hard at any tier. Interval coverage is 0.575 and 0.400 over 40 targets, which is poor; the runbook's caution that this is not a coverage estimate applies, but it is much worse than VG15's.

**The third replicate was not run.** The run was stopped to save an hour of sampling once the first two agreed. Two replicates is thin, and the claim above rests on their agreeing with each other and with the three `test`-tier replicates.

## VG11 at `test`: the bias runs the other way, which is a problem for the mechanism

Only **1 of 3** replicates cleared the gate (R-hat 1.0093, then 1.0153 and 1.0120), so VG11 needs `rep` exactly as VG15 did and nothing here is conclusive. The direction is nonetheless consistent across all three, and it is the opposite of VG12's:

| Quantity                 | r01 z (assessed) | r02 z | r03 z | Inside 89%?        |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- | ----- | ----- | ------------------ |
| `v_total`                | **+3.52**        | +1.93 | +1.27 | no, no, yes        |
| `subject_variance_share` | +0.11            | +1.34 | +0.84 | **yes, all three** |
| `tau_subject`            | **+3.23**        | +2.74 | +1.78 | **no, all three**  |

**VG11 and VG12 carry the same variance partition and their between-child scales are biased in opposite directions, by different routes.** In VG12 the budget is right and the split is wrong; in VG11 the split is right and the budget is wrong. Both produce a mis-recovered `tau_subject` because it is the square root of their product, but only one of them is the mechanism #225 names.

That matters for #225's framing. Its summary attributes the bias to `subject_variance_share` and asks whether the partition causes it. On this evidence the partition is **not sufficient** to produce a downward bias — VG11 has it and recovers the share cleanly — so either the mechanism is model-specific or `subject_variance_share` is a symptom in VG12 rather than the cause. The obvious next test is the one #225 already frames: refit VG12 without the partition and see whether `tau_subject` recovers.

It also bears on the Down syndrome / typically developing contrast the comparison book withdrew a claim about. That book's cancellation argument assumed a shared bias on both sides. Two typically developing models now show biases of opposite sign, so "the typically developing side's bias" is not a single number, and which model the contrast reads matters.

## A harness defect this exposed

`recovery_matrix_<model>.csv` is assembled from whatever replicate directories exist under `output/models/` and **records no sampling configuration per replicate**. When the VG12 `rep` run stopped after two replicates, the third directory still held the `test`-tier fit from that morning, and the matrix pooled it with the two `rep` replicates and published a mixed-tier `POOLED (2 of 3 replicates assessed)` row. Passing `--replicate 1 --replicate 2` to the scoring stage did not prevent it; the scoring stage's replicate selection does not restrict what the matrix assembles.

The stale directory is quarantined under `output/failed/VG12-age-understood-td-re-recovery-r03-test-tier-superseded/` with a note recording why, and its simulated data and truth are untouched under `output/recovery/`, so the `rep` fit can be run later without re-simulating. The matrix now reads `POOLED (2 of 2 replicates assessed)`.

**The fix belongs in the harness, not in operator discipline**: the matrix should record each replicate's `config_name` from its fit manifest and refuse to pool across tiers, the same way `fit_identity` refuses to accept a fit whose definition has moved. Registered as task 4.7 of #289.

## Caveats

- Two replicates for VG12 and one assessable replicate for VG11 are thin. Nothing here is simulation-based calibration, and `coverage_ci89` is not a coverage estimate — the runbook is explicit that targets within a replicate are strongly correlated functions of one truth.
- Truths are drawn from each model of record's own posterior, so these studies ask whether the sampler can find parameters _in the region the data put them_, not whether it could find any parameters.
- VG15 and VG12 are `rep`-tier; VG11 is `test`-tier and its replicates mostly did not converge. Comparing VG11's numbers with VG12's is comparing across tiers, which is exactly the confusion the harness defect above created — stated here so the comparison is made knowingly.
- VG15 is a Down syndrome model, so its fit of record and this recovery study both go stale when `us_03` is ingested. The finding is about identifiability at this data size and shape, which the new study changes: 186 more children, none of them signing.
