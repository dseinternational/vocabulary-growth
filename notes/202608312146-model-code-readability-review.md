## Provenance and what is verified

Findings are marked `verified` where a verifier confirmed the claim against the code, and `unverified` where no verifier entry matched the finding — those are flagged individually and are directly checkable. Four claims were additionally re-checked by hand when this note was written and all four held exactly: `x_center_z` is passed at one of seven `GPGrid(...)` construction sites (`common.py:873`); `diff` of `common_bivariate.py:723-789` against `common_bivariate_re.py:1221-1287` differs on one line, a section banner; the post-cast count checks are dead and the code says so twelve lines above them (`common_bivariate.py:437-441`); and the stale counts are as reported — eight occurrences of "all seventeen models", two of "fifteen models", and "six models of record" at `definitions.py:1117` for a class tree that holds twelve, against twenty registered models.

## Verdict

The model code is unusually well _explained_ and structurally behind its own explanations. At the statement level this is some of the best-commented scientific Python I have reviewed: the load-bearing decisions — the HSGP half-range derivation (`src/vocab_growth/models/common.py:580`), the branch-free Plackett identity and why the textbook root cancels near psi=1 (`src/vocab_growth/models/common_joint_modality.py:932`), the degenerate-LOO threshold (`src/vocab_growth/models/common.py:1196`), the zero-sum identifiability constraint (`src/vocab_growth/models/common_bivariate_re.py:887`), the FILTERED-spec ordering hazard (`src/vocab_growth/models/common_joint_modality.py:1031`) — are recorded where a reader meets them, with issue numbers and measurements. Curation is visible: `catalogue.py`, `fit_identity.py`, `subject_effects.py`, `observation_arrays.py`, `reporting_ages.py` and `build_utils.py` are all deliberate extractions from exactly the defects they now prevent, and `tests/test_graph_equivalence.py` mechanises byte-identity for every registered model.

Two things are genuinely wrong across the whole surface. First, the _sequences_ that call the well-designed shared helpers are still hand-written per engine family — build prologue and epilogue, the kappa and grid Deterministic blocks, the likelihood tail, the posterior-predictive tail, the per-outcome prior-check, summary and plot stages each exist in four to seven copies — and three of those copies have already drifted in ways nothing detects. Second, the prose a newcomer reads first is the prose most out of date: two engine docstrings state a study-intercept prior neither engine builds, one licenses the copy-paste that half these findings are made of, one points at the wrong builder for twelve of thirteen models, and the escape hatch that decides whether a change costs a multi-gigabyte refit is described in all three agent-instruction files as never having been used. Nothing here threatens a currently reported number except two defects confined to VG14 (superseded for reporting) and one violated invariant — the HSGP basis-centre pin — which is a statistical decision rather than a bug. Almost all of the recommended work is code motion; the two hardest items have a ready-made proof in the graph-equivalence baseline.

## What is in good shape

The shared layer is real and genuinely used: no engine family open-codes the trend/GP construction or the kappa dispatch, `run_fit_pipeline` / `_shared_diagnostics` / `emit_monthly_summary` / `build_kappa_for_config` are common property, and the `build_utils` ↔ `gp_utils` boundary is a stated, principled rule (pure NumPy vs emits PyMC ops, so `definitions.py` need not import pymc) rather than an accident.

`catalogue.py` did what it was built for: it removed a seven-way engine-dispatch duplication in which three of seven copies were wrong at once, engine identity is declared rather than inferred (VG05 and VG07 share a definition class and different engines), the record is deliberately outside the serialised definition, and `tests/test_model_catalogue.py` pins every declaration against the code it describes in both directions. `fit_identity.py`'s field classification is complete, fails closed, and its `BACKFILL_DEFAULTS` entries carry checkable claims about history. All twenty `model_vgNN.py` wrappers are genuinely uniform with no unexplained divergence. `subject_effects.py` and `observation_arrays.py` are model extractions whose docstrings name the specific past failures they close.

`tests/test_graph_equivalence.py` — free-RV names and order, deterministic names, likelihood factorisation, dims/coords and a fixed-point logp for every registered model — is the single most valuable asset for the work below: it converts most of Tier 2 from a judgement call into a test run. `reporting_ages.py` is a proper policy module with a named-quantity API, `definitions.py`'s prior constants are named systematically enough that "which prior does VG12 use for understood kappa" is a two-hop lookup, and the hard constraints on the definition surface (the overloaded `tau_subj_*_sigma` seam, the sibling subclasses, `CLAMP_Q_ONLY`, `_as_definition_subclass`) are each justified where they are used. There are no bare `except`s and no `assert`s used for validation anywhere in `src/`; SPDX headers and informative module docstrings are universal. The exploratory package's non-validatable status is structural, not prose-only.

## Findings

### 1. The #234 HSGP basis-centre pin was applied in one of seven `GPGrid` construction sites, and the invariant `fit_identity` relies on is violated for sixteen models

**Claim.** `common.py:861-873` passes `x_center_z=float(np.mean(grids.X_gp_domain_z))` with a six-line rationale; the other six construction sites (`common_bivariate.py:594`, `common_bivariate_re.py:830`, `common_trivariate.py:705`, `common_joint_modality.py:1246`, `common_univariate_re.py:447`, `exploratory/vg17.py:224`) pass only `sa_z, sb_z, ell_low_z, ell_high_z, M, L`, so `gp_utils.py:1006` lets PyMC centre the basis lazily on the min/max of the stacked `[obs, plot, query, anchor]` grid. Five of the six already compute and pass `grids.X_gp_domain_z` to `get_hsgp_hyperparams`, so the value the pin needs is in scope and unused; `exploratory/vg17.py:197` is on the pre-#234 form and takes even `L` and `M` from the reporting grid.

**Why it matters.** The whole point of separating `X_gp_domain_z` from `X_all_z` was that a reporting-query change must not move the HSGP approximation, and `fit_identity.py:134-139` classifies `ages_query` as `REPORTING` explicitly on the grounds that "the likelihood never touches either … The posterior is identical." That is true only where the pin is applied. Unpinned, `_X_center` is a function of the extremes of `X_all_z`, which include `X_query_z`, so adding or removing a query age moves the basis and therefore the likelihood — the measured VG04 example in `notes/202608231145-vg01-vg04-statistical-model-review.md:94` shifted the centre from 19 to 16.5 months against an explicit 8-30 domain, and VG04 is on the one engine that got the fix. Sixteen of twenty models are on the unpinned engines, and the twelve lines that would tell a maintainer the hazard exists live only in `common.py`.

**Recommendation.** Land the mechanism first: give `AgeGrids` a `gp_center_z` field computed in `construct_age_grids`, or add a `build_utils.gp_grid_for(grids, *, sa_z, sb_z, ell_low_z, ell_high_z, M, L)` factory that every engine calls, so omitting the pin becomes impossible. Then decide per model from the `tests/test_graph_equivalence.py` baseline diff: models whose midpoints already coincide are pinned for free; any model whose logp moves needs a recorded reporting-quality refit, not a silent change. Fix `vg17.py:197` to take `grids.X_gp_domain_z` in the same change, but note that `exploratory/vg17.py:158-168` records a study-owner decision of 2026-08-31 not to widen its GP domain — the switch may move vg17's `L` and `M` and should be stated as such.

**Blast radius:** needs-refit · **Severity:** high · **Confidence:** verified

### 2. Docstrings misstate the prior, the engine and the data sources — in the four places a new maintainer reads first

**Claim.** Four separate misstatements, each at the top of a file or a term. (a) `common_bivariate_re.py:14-17` and `common_univariate_re.py:12` declare an independent-Normal study-intercept prior (`delta[s] ~ Normal(0, tau)`, "the same distribution as above (see issue #65)"); both engines build `pm.ZeroSumNormal(sigma=sqrt(K/(K-1)))`, and the in-body comments forty lines down say the opposite and are correct ("an intentional identifiability constraint, NOT a prior-preserving reparameterisation", `common_bivariate_re.py:887`, `common_univariate_re.py:474`). (b) `common_bivariate.py:6` and `:2547` say the module and `fit_bivariate_model` serve "VG05, VG07-VG10, VG13" when the catalogue routes all but VG05 to `common_bivariate_re`, so the file's most prominent code — `build_model`, 365 lines — has the smallest reach in it, while `_run_bivariate_joint_plots`'s docstring is stale the other way ("shared by VG05–VG07" for the plot stage of all thirteen). (c) `common_trivariate.py:22-24` states "This module is a deliberate, self-contained copy-and-extend of common_bivariate.py … some duplication is intentional", while the module imports fourteen names from `common`, aliases `sample` and `diagonostics` to the shared ones, and defers `TrivariateModelSamples`' rationale to the bivariate class; line 20's "VG15 will relax it" is contradicted by line 2164 in the same file. (d) `common_joint_modality.py:1563`, `:1585` and `:1814` label the multi-source four-cell Dirichlet-Multinomial and its PPC extraction "uk_02" when `idx_cells` covers uk_02, uk_07 and es_01 — the exact mislabelling #238 was raised to fix in the figure layer — and `:530` omits es_01 from the cross-tab source list it appends to identically at `:538`.

**Why it matters.** (a) is the first statement a reader gets about each RE engine's prior, and it is the version that ends up paraphrased into report prose; anyone reasoning about identifiability or exchangeability will take it at face value. (b) sends someone asked to change how VG10 assembles its likelihood to the wrong builder, and makes them over-cautious about a function serving one model. (c) actively licenses the copy-paste that findings 3, 6, 7 and 8 are made of, and hides the shared helpers they should be reaching for. (d) matters because the per-source Mantel-Haenszel odds ratios recorded at `common_joint_modality.py:1468` are uk_02 6.09, uk_07 13.90, nz_01 14.63, es_01 0.90 — "which sources are in this term" is the single most consequential fact about the psi likelihood, and the module's own docstring at line 43 already gets it right, so the file disagrees with itself.

**Recommendation.** Rewrite the two RE prior blocks to state what is built (`delta_raw ~ ZeroSumNormal(sqrt(K/(K-1)))`, `delta = tau * delta_raw`, marginal per-study variance tau², group mean removed, −1/K correlation imposed) and point at the in-body comment for the argument rather than restating it; do not shorten the in-body copies. Correct `common_bivariate`'s three docstrings against the catalogue, naming `catalogue.ENGINES` as the authority. Replace `common_trivariate`'s licence-to-duplicate paragraph with what is genuinely engine-specific (the three-anchor tent mean for r, `p_any` under conditional independence, two nested outcomes sharing one comprehension denominator, the uk_02 union check) plus the shared modules everything else comes from — and note that `observation_arrays` is the one this engine pointedly does not use. Retitle the four joint comments to the multi-source reality, keeping every rationale sentence. Extend `common_univariate_re`'s docstring to name the centred-study branch, the subject random effect, the variance partition and singleton marginalisation.

**Blast radius:** safe · **Severity:** high · **Confidence:** (a)(b) unverified — no verifier entry matched, though the code claims are checkable in minutes; (c)(d) verified

### 3. Sixty-five byte-identical lines end both bivariate `build_model` bodies, including the comment asserting they cannot drift

**Claim.** `diff <(sed -n '723,789p' common_bivariate.py) <(sed -n '1221,1287p' common_bivariate_re.py)` differs on one line: a section banner. The 65 shared lines cover the `kappa_s` deterministics, the entire understood likelihood (`p_u_obs_sel` → clip → `alpha_u`/`beta_u` → `pm.BetaBinomial("y_u_obs", …)`), the `nested_outcome_alpha_beta` call, `y_s_obs`, and the build epilogue. The understood block appears a third time at `common_trivariate.py:948` and in a materially different, non-shareable form at `common_joint_modality.py:1516`.

**Why it matters.** Every change to the observation likelihood must be made twice in two files 500 lines apart, and `tests/test_graph_equivalence.py` catches a change _from its own baseline_, not a divergence _between_ engines: a fix applied to one engine with its baseline updated leaves the other passing unchanged. That is precisely the #266-finding-3 shape recorded at `common_trivariate.py:557-562`, where the same latent defect survived unreachably because nobody propagated the fix.

**Recommendation.** Extract `emit_nested_bivariate_likelihoods(*, config, definition, X_obs_mean, X_obs_std, z_obs, z_plot, z_query, p_u_obs, q_obs, p_s_obs, idx_u, idx_s, n_trials, s_likelihood_n, s_is_conditional, y_u_observed, y_s_observed, spoken_fallback, hold_kappa_u=False, hold_kappa_q=False)` into a new `models/likelihood_blocks.py` and call it from both bivariate engines. The `hold_kappa_*` parameters are load-bearing — the RE engine passes `hold_constant=spec_q is not None and spec_q.hold_kappa_constant` at `common_bivariate_re.py:1215`, which is why the identical region starts _after_ `build_kappa_for_config`. Do not fold in the trivariate or joint copies: the joint engine computes `marginal_p=(p_u_obs * q_obs)[idx_s]` inline, stores no kappa grid deterministics, and uses different local names. If the helper goes in `likelihood_utils.py` instead, import pymc inside the function — that module is deliberately pymc-free at import so `definitions.py` can use it. Creation order and every variable name are already identical, so the graph-equivalence baseline is the byte-identity proof for VG05 and VG07-VG23 in one run. Drop the "Both bivariate engines route through the one helper so their graphs cannot drift apart" line of argument (`common_bivariate.py:755`): it refers correctly to `nested_outcome_alpha_beta`; the 65-line diff carries the finding on its own.

**Blast radius:** needs-care · **Severity:** high · **Confidence:** verified

### 5. "Which child-effect structure is this?" is answered four ways in three vocabularies; the typed resolver has one caller

**Claim.** `subject_effects.resolve(definition)` returns a typed `SubjectEffectPlan` over `SubjectEffectKind` = {NONE, CONSTANT, VARIANCE_PARTITION, AGE_VARYING, CHILD_SLOPE, FACTOR} plus `correlation_eta` / `factor` / `slope_ref_age_months`, and refuses every illegal combination before the model context is entered. It has exactly one production caller, `common_bivariate_re.py:595`. Three other sites re-derive the same fact: the predictive path name-sniffs the built model through four selectors (`common_bivariate.py:1104`, `:1118`, `:1202`, plus `model_variables.get("rho_uq")`) in an if/elif chain written out twice, once for `u` and once for `q`; `prior_child_checks.child_effect_structure` (`prior_child_checks.py:87`) sniffs the _prior_ dataset and returns the magic strings `"factor"`/`"slope"`/`"correlated"`/`"independent"`/`"none"` — with no case for VARIANCE_PARTITION or AGE_VARYING — while never reading its `definition` parameter at all; and `pair_plot_priority` (`common_bivariate.py:1156`) duck-types a slope with `getattr(spec, "tau1_sigma", None) is not None` rather than calling `definitions.subject_slope_spec`. `common_univariate_re.py:502-513` re-derives the variance-partition case from `config.has_variance_partition` and re-raises, _inside_ `with pm.Model()`, the refusal `subject_effects.py:261-266` already makes. The factor-before-slope precedence is documented three times (`common_bivariate.py:1206`, `prior_child_checks.py:90`, `common_bivariate.py:1122`) and nothing says the same precedence must now hold in three independent implementations.

**Why it matters.** Adding a sixth structure, or changing the precedence, means four edits in four idioms, three of them reached only by running a fit or a plot, and three of them failing silently rather than raising — a block that happens to emit `tau_subj_u_1` gets VG19's unseen-child construction with no error. The dangerous _definition_ combinations are already closed by the resolver's refusals and `tests/test_subject_effect_plan.py` covers every registered model, so this is a parallel-maintenance hazard rather than a live defect; but the taxonomy is already inconsistent across its copies, and the univariate refusal fires part-way through a half-built graph, which is exactly what #273 removed for the bivariate engine.

**Recommendation.** Make the resolved plan the single input: pass the whole `SubjectEffectPlan` (not a bare `SubjectEffectKind` — VG20's correlation is a plan-level attribute, `subject_effects.py:143`, so the enum alone cannot select its branch) into `sample_posterior_predictive`, `pair_plot_priority` and `prior_child_checks.run`, and branch on it; keep the name lookups only for _fetching_ variables a branch already knows exist. Collapse the two duplicated outcome branches into one `_unseen_child_deltas(context, plan, outcome, correlated_with=...)`. Call `resolve` from `common_univariate_re.build_model` before the model context and delete the duplicated raise at `:509-513`. Either drop `child_effect_structure`'s dead `definition` parameter or, better, derive its answer from the plan and keep the prior-name checks as an assertion that the trace matches. Gate on `tests/test_graph_equivalence.py`: the predictive path creates RVs (`_z_factor_marg`, `_z0_subj_u_marg`, `_delta_subj_q_marg`), so branch selection must be argued equivalent.

**Blast radius:** needs-care · **Severity:** high · **Confidence:** verified as to the four idioms and the univariate duplication; **lower confidence** on two unverified sub-claims worth checking during the work — that `prior_child_checks`' missing AGE_VARYING case makes an A1 sensitivity fit (`sensitivity/registry.py:301`) draw constant prior child offsets against age-varying predictive ones, and that `_z_subj_q_marg` names the correlated innovation at `common_bivariate.py:1233` and the child's own deviate at `:1404`. The "Checked first" comment at `:1322` describes a branch checked second.

### The verification pass, and the one defect it caught

The finished change set was put to five independent adversarial lenses (definitions, validation, deletions, claims, behaviour) plus a judge, all instructed to default to refutation and to run code rather than read it. Twenty-eight candidate defects; the judge discarded five as pre-existing or misread and confirmed the rest.

**One was blocking, and was mine.** Tier 1 item 12 added an assertion that `run_fit_pipeline`'s stage 0 is named `PREPARE_STAGE_NAME`, because the fit manifest is written straight after it and needs the prepared frame. But `recovery/refit.py` and `scripts/experiments/vg10_under_vg20_truth.py` deliberately _substitute_ stage 0 with a loader for a simulated frame — `"Load simulated data"` — so every `scripts/fit_recovery.py` fit died after its loader stage had run, retaining a failed fit. The fast suite passed with the harness broken. The guard now checks the stage's **effect** (`context.has_prepared_data`) rather than its name, which still catches the case it was written for — an engine opening with the priors stage, which sets neither field — and `tests/test_fit_artifacts.py` gained the missing regression test. The lesson generalises: an assertion about a _name_ is an assertion about a convention, and two callers had a good reason to break the convention while keeping the contract.

The rest were documentation, and all are fixed. The ones worth recording as facts rather than edits:

- **ZeroSumNormal's off-diagonal correlation is `-1/(K-1)`, not `-1/K`** — confirmed empirically at K = 4, 5, 8 (200k draws each: −0.3333, −0.2500, −0.1429). `-1/K` is the _covariance_ of a `ZeroSumNormal(1)` before that rescaling. The wrong figure was in two module docstrings and three construction-site comments.
- **`clamp_mean_above_hi_anchor` is declared by 14 of the 20 registered models**, not all twenty — that is `report_max_age_understood`'s reach. Both are now asserted in `tests/test_ds_joint_shared_priors.py`.
- **The `BivariateModelDefinition` tree holds 12 models, 8 of them direct instances.** Both numbers are asserted; the second is the one a maintainer mistakes for the refit bill.
- **VG23 derives from the typically-developing VG13, not from VG10**, so it does not inherit the DS-calibrated joint anchors. A comment naming it among those that do was the single place a reader learns which models the DS anchors reach.
- **VG14 declares no `use_subject_re_*` field at all**, so the DS-joint group's shared property is "fitted to this frame", not "carries subject intercepts on both outcomes". Its own registration already recorded that as a KNOWN MISMATCH.
- **The 72-84 month comprehension band holds 24 rows from 18 children**, not 25 from 20: the `uk_01` correction of 2026-08-31 removed one, and three comments still carried the old figure. Nothing about the reporting-cap argument depends on it.
- **`recovery.spec.supported_models()` went from 11 to 13** when VG21-VG23 were registered, which staled the enumeration in all three agent-instruction copies and in `docs/runbooks/parameter-recovery.md`. Those now point at `supported_models()` / `UNSUPPORTED_REASONS`, which a guard proves partition the registry.

One substantive gap surfaced that was not in the review: `build_univariate_re_model` re-read its outcome column with `dtype=int` and no range check, and `recovery/simulate.py` re-enters that builder twice on a _simulated_ frame no prepare stage has seen. VG11 and VG12 are recovery targets, so the path is live. It now validates on the pre-cast float inside the builder, as three of the six engines already did — a no-op on the normal fit path.

## Where the code misled a reviewer

The refuted list is empty: no finding was rejected outright. But the verifiers rescoped nineteen findings, and in most of those cases the reviewer's error was not carelessness — it was a deliberate decision that is invisible at the site where the code has to be read. Those are documentation gaps of exactly the kind this project otherwise closes well.

`prepare_bivariate_observations` reads `analysis_df["study_code"]` and `n_studies` unconditionally (`observation_arrays.py:182`), and the column is created only by the RE engine's own prepare stage. Two reviewers independently proposed a one-line call from `common_bivariate.build_model` that would `KeyError`, and one proposed generalising the function for the trivariate case, which would refactor six models' builder. Nothing in the helper's docstring states its frame preconditions or that its holdout and study/subject-code handling is RE-specific.

`common_trivariate.py:768`'s `getattr(definition, "sign_peak_prior", None)` looks identical to probes elsewhere that are provably redundant, and one reviewer proposed removing it. It is required: `TrivariateModelDefinition` has no such field, so the plain read raises on every VG14 build. The exemplary counter-example is in the same family — `common.py:882` carries a comment saying exactly why its probe can never hit — which is what makes the unexplained probes at `common.py:1540` and `:1970` read as house style.

The trivariate plot helper's missing `subject_ids` / `form_max` / `trajectory_samples` was read as drift by two reviewers. It is correct: `TrivariateModelDefinition` carries no random intercepts, the graph has no `*_subject_marginal` node, and the frame has neither `subject_key` nor `survey_vocab_max`. Nothing at the site says the three keywords are a `bivariate_re` feature that VG05 also does not get.

`common_univariate_re.py:313`'s post-cast range check looks identical to three unreachable copies and is the only range check that column gets. `common_trivariate.summary_s`' missing trim looks like the oversight #238 found five times elsewhere and is a provable no-op because `max_age_for(SPOKEN)` returns `max(ages_query)`. `common.posterior_summary`' omitted `max_age_months` looks like an oversight three sibling engines avoid and is deliberate, recorded only in `docs/models/vg02/index.qmd:268`. In all three cases the missing artefact is one sentence at the call site.

The plot-stage/summary-stage boundary is load-bearing — `regenerate_plots.py` re-runs only the plot stage, so a table moved into `posterior_summary` converts a cheap replot into a reporting-quality refit — and that fact is recorded only in a `KNOWN_STALE` comment inside `tests/test_reporting_age_policy.py`. A reviewer accordingly proposed moving two VG15 tables the wrong way.

`fit_identity._GRAPH_PREFIXES` reads as a classifier (its neighbouring comment says "matched by prefix for the families that are…") and is a completeness whitelist behind a dead branch. `_as_definition_subclass` is this repository's documented mechanism for adding a field without invalidating a parent's fits, but it is defined 3,600 lines in under a `# VG20` banner and is not mentioned at any of the `getattr` probe sites that exist because of the constraint it relieves — so a reviewer concluded that adding `sign_gp_mode` must cost every VG15 fit. Likewise, `clamp_mean_above_hi_anchor`'s `GRAPH` classification in `fit_identity.py:149` is what actually prevents the hazard a reviewer feared (a field added quietly turning the clamp on); the classification is invisible from the probe.

`vg17`'s duplicate kappa translation was attributed to the leading underscore on `_configure_kappa_priors`. The real cause is that the shared function takes a `ModelFitContext` and emits figures into it, while vg17 needs the translation at module level before any context exists — and the vg17 docstring claims the reuse is genuine. Nothing at either site says the coupling is what forced the copy.

`plotting._save_png_svg` is private, so a reviewer's recommendation that engine modules route through it would have reproduced the very cross-module private-import smell another finding is about. And `regenerate_plots.py:210`'s comment about which engines expose a pure extractor is simply wrong for eleven models, because the branch is decided by an engine module's import list rather than by any declaration — so two reviewers got the affected count wrong in opposite directions.

Two places where the documentation _worked_ and are worth preserving as the model: `exploratory/vg17.py:158-168` records a dated study-owner decision not to widen the GP domain, which stopped a reviewer's incidental cleanup from becoming a statistical change; and `scripts/sign_speech_association_audit.py:97-99` records why four uk_02 rows have a `comprehension` value above their own cells, which is the only reason anyone can tell finding 14's denominator change is a decision rather than a fix.

## Not worth doing

**Splitting `gp_utils.py` into three modules.** The file's membership rule is explicit and honoured — "helpers that emit PyMC ops … so the pure module stays `pymc`-free" (`gp_utils.py:6`) — and at 1025 lines it is mid-sized in this repository. The real harm is discoverability: `subject_effects.py` tabulates five child-effect structures and holds none of their builders. Take the cheap half (a three-group index in the docstring plus a cross-reference from `subject_effects.py` naming `build_child_factor`, `build_child_slope`, `build_subject_scale_of_z` and `build_variance_partition`) and stop. Do **not** move the kappa builders to a new module: they are imported only by `common.py`, the two wrappers exist to wrap `make_kappa_of_z` and belong beside it, and `kappa_grid_block` would have to live in `common.py` anyway to avoid a cycle. The child-effect group is the one part with a natural home and only two import sites, so split that only if the docstring fix proves insufficient.

**Turning `definitions.py` into a package.** The 4,116-line file does mix four audiences, but the concrete cost identified — a misplaced helper — is fixed by moving one function, and the split would churn 118 importing modules behind a re-export shim for an organisational preference. Take the helper move (Tier 1) and the duplication fix (finding 4), which together remove ~1,900 lines of restated text and are where the maintenance risk actually is.

**Adding `RegisteredModel.fit` to derive the dispatch.** `scripts/fit_model.py:280` already resolves the wrapper through the catalogue and the missing-wrapper failure is a self-explaining `ModuleNotFoundError` naming the dotted path; the pair is already pinned by `test_fit_dispatches_to_the_declared_engine_with_the_registered_definition`. Worse, four experiment scripts (`scripts/experiments/clamp_arm.py:60`, `geom_arm.py:61`, `vg12_eta_isolate.py:26`, `vg13_ell_arm.py:57`) work by rebinding the wrapper's module global, which a catalogue-resolved definition would silently bypass. Fix the documentation instead.

**Moving `signing_milestones.csv` and `signed_ratio_peak.csv` into `posterior_summary`.** Refuted on the mechanics: the plot stage is the only one `regenerate_plots.py` re-runs, so this converts a replot into a multi-gigabyte refit the next time a cap moves. Fix the discoverability complaint with a named step (`_write_signing_tables`) and a docstring line.

**Collapsing the bivariate and trivariate joint-trajectory figures into one shared function.** The cap fix and the CSV routing (finding 13) are free and should be done. The collapse must carry band alphas, scatter alpha and title per series and per figure or it silently restyles the published figures of six models, and the payoff is one fewer near-duplicate in a family that will have several fewer after findings 3, 7 and 8. Revisit after those land.

**Renaming `tau_u_sigma` / `tau_q_sigma` / `tau_sign_sigma` for clarity.** They are serialised definition fields, so a rename is needs-refit for seventeen models, and the confusion with `tau_subj_u_sigma` one line away is better addressed by the suffix legend and a `STUDY_SCALE_FIELDS` mapping. Explicitly do not do this.

**Collapsing `ModelFitContext`'s setters onto a descriptor.** Descriptors fight dataclass field defaults, `common.py` is outside mypy's scope so corrected annotations will not be checked either, and the actual value is in the class docstring and the `_require` helper's message naming the model. Take those two; leave the shape alone.

**Making `common_bivariate_re` use a pure sample extractor.** It would remove one `getattr` probe and change eleven models from re-sampling their posterior predictive to reusing stored draws during a replot. That is a behavioural decision about what `regenerate_plots.py` guarantees, not a tidy-up, and it should not ride along with finding 17's declaration fix.
