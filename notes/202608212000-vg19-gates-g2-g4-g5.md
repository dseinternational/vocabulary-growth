## 4. G4's headline diagnostic — refuted

The plan: _"does `kappa_u`'s 24→48 month decline shrink once the child slope can absorb drift? That number is the whole reason for the exercise."_

Posterior median `kappa_u` on the plot grid:

| fit              |     24 mo |     48 mo | ratio 48/24 |
| ---------------- | --------: | --------: | ----------: |
| VG10 (parent)    |     64.82 |     24.41 |       0.377 |
| VG20 (record)    |     61.58 |     24.68 |       0.401 |
| **VG19 (slope)** | **73.39** | **24.36** |   **0.332** |

**It steepened.** The decline is _larger_ under the slope, not smaller.

This was checked for the obvious confound before being believed: `kappa_u` and `kappa_s` are byte-identical across all three definitions, all three were fitted 19-21 August under the promoted `kappa-anchor-18-72-floor` block, and all three carry `n_trials = 810`. The difference is real.

The mechanism is coherent rather than noise, which is what makes it informative. `kappa_u` **rose** at 24 months (73.4 against VG10's 64.8 — less residual dispersion, so the slope did absorb variation there) and is unchanged at 48 (24.36 against 24.41). That is what the algebra predicts: with `rho01` = −0.219 and a reference age of 36 months, the slope _adds_ between-child variance at 24 months (2·rho·tau0·tau1·D + tau1²D² = +0.089 at D = −1) and slightly _removes_ it at 48 (−0.027 at D = +1). The child slope explains dispersion where it was asked to and not where the decline lives.

**So `kappa_u`'s decline is not unmodelled child drift.** The plan anticipated an informative negative here and this is one. It removes the leading candidate explanation and sends [#229](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/229) and the mechanism hunt (#49) elsewhere.

The variant caps the data at 84 months and changes **nothing else** — deliberately unlike `window-22`, which moves the GP domain and anchors with the cap, because doing that here would let `tau1` move for a reason other than the discarded rows.

| ---------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- | -----: | -----------: |
| `tau_subj_u_1`   | 0.176 [0.092, 0.242] | 0.214 [0.148, 0.274] | +0.038 |        0.047 |
| `tau_subj_q_1`   | 0.640 [0.538, 0.743] | 0.675 [0.570, 0.781] | +0.035 |        0.064 |
| `tau_subj_u_rho` | −0.219               | −0.228               | −0.009 |            — |
| `tau_subj_q_rho` | 0.469                | 0.483                | +0.014 |            — |

**`tau1` does not move materially.** Both shifts are under one posterior standard deviation (0.8 and 0.55) and the intervals overlap heavily.

The **direction** is what retires the concern rather than merely failing to confirm it. Had the 50 spoken rows above 84 months been manufacturing the rate, discarding them would have driven `tau1` toward zero. It rose slightly instead, which is the signature of a parameter carried by the longitudinal subset while the high-age rows add noise around it. Convergence improved too — R-hat 1.0047 against 1.0092, minimum ESS 1617 against 822, zero divergences — consistent with the discarded points stretching the geometry rather than informing it.

The leverage calculation in the plan was therefore correct about the _concentration_ and wrong about its _consequence_: 3.5% of the spoken data does carry 28-35% of the cross-sectional information about `tau1`, but the rate is not hostage to it. Both caveats stated alongside that table — that it covers only the cross-sectional route, and that it ignores ceiling attenuation — point the same way, and this fit is the measurement that settles it.

## 4c. VG19 against VG20 directly

§3 gated VG19 against VG10, its nesting parent. That leaves the question the promotion actually poses — VG20 is the model of record, so how do the two candidates for the child-effect structure compare with each other? They are **not nested in either direction**, so there is no likelihood-ratio route: VG19 adds a per-outcome age slope and forces `rho_uq` to zero; VG20 estimates `rho_uq` and holds the child effect flat in age. Paired pointwise LOO on the same observations is the only comparison available, and all three fits share the same data, the same promoted `kappa` block and 36,000 draws, so the pairing is legitimate.

| outcome    | pair      |   diff |   SE |          |
| ---------- | --------- | -----: | ---: | -------- |
| spoken     | VG19-VG20 | +119.5 | 17.4 | +6.86 SE |
| spoken     | VG20-VG10 |  +12.6 |  7.2 | +1.75 SE |
| understood | VG19-VG20 |  +18.3 |  6.7 | +2.75 SE |
| understood | VG20-VG10 |   −1.8 |  5.9 | −0.30 SE |

**The two extensions are not comparable in size.** Adding the cross-outcome correlation bought essentially nothing in held-out prediction — under one and a half standard errors on spoken words and nothing at all on understood, despite `rho_uq` being estimated at +0.368 with an interval well clear of zero. Adding the child slope buys roughly ten times as much on spoken words. This does not undermine the VG20 promotion, which was made on interpretive grounds — `rho_uq` is needed to carry the DS/TD between-child derivation, and a parameter can be real and reportable without improving prediction — but it does mean the promotion should not be cited as a predictive result, and it locates almost all of the available predictive headroom in the slope rather than the correlation.

### What `rho_uq` is doing, and why zeroing it is not a free simplification

The LOO table above makes `rho_uq` look expendable — it buys +1.75 SE on spoken and nothing on understood — so it is worth being explicit about what VG19 gives up by pinning it at zero, because the answer is not "a parameter that does not pay for itself".

Spoken is `u * q`. A child's spoken position is therefore a function of **both** random effects at once, and the between-child spread in spoken words depends on whether those two effects compound or partly cancel. `rho_uq` = +0.368 [0.287, 0.447] says they compound: a child who understands more than their peers also tends to say more of what they understand. Forcing it to zero does not remove a nuisance term, it asserts a specific and contrary fact about children — that the two are independent — and the consequence is quantified in [`docs/models/README.md`](../docs/models/README.md): the correlation widens the spoken subject-marginal intervals by **9-33%**, most at the youngest ages. The DS/TD between-child derivation reads it too (`comparison.py:916`); under a VG19 model of record that computation would revert to the independent path it was changed away from.

That is also why "VG20 was promoted on interpretive rather than predictive grounds" is the right description and not a hedge. `rho_uq` earns its place by fixing the width of an individual-level interval, and a parameter can do that while barely moving an aggregate held-out score — LOO scores a point prediction at observed ages, and the interval is a different functional of the same posterior.

**The correction this forces.** It is tempting to summarise the position as "VG19 is the narrower model because it drops the correlation", and that is wrong. §4 of [202608220748](202608220748-vg19-individual-trajectories.md) computes the spoken spread under both and finds they **cross at about 48 months**: VG20's correlated-but-constant effects give the wider individual band below that age, VG19's growing-but-uncorrelated effects give it above, and VG20's band narrows with age on the natural scale purely through ceiling compression. Neither model is the conservative choice across the reported range. The 4x4 successor is not a tidiness exercise — it is the only structure in which both mechanisms are present at once, and until it exists any single-model individual interval is too narrow at one end of the age range or the other.

### Why the LOO margin is not yet a prediction claim

Two separate objections apply, and only one of them is about the approximation.

**The approximation is more strained for the winner.** PSIS-LOO reweights the full-posterior draws to approximate leaving each observation out; Pareto `k` is the fitted tail shape of those importance weights, and `k` > 0.7 means the weights have effectively infinite variance at practical draw counts, so that observation's contribution is unreliable and typically **optimistic** — the full posterior has already seen the point being predicted. VG19 carries 399 such observations on the spoken outcome against VG20's 304, and `p_loo` 744.8 against 646.6. The bias runs in the direction of the winner, which is the configuration in which a LOO margin is least trustworthy.

**Leave-one-_observation_-out is the wrong unit here, and this objection is structural rather than numerical.** The pool holds 1,428 spoken observations on 767 children — 1.86 per child. Dropping one observation usually leaves the same child's other observation in the fit, which pins that child's random effect, so LOO is largely measuring how well each model predicts a child's _second_ visit given their first. That is not the quantity the study reports on. It also does not favour the two models equally: VG19 carries four per-child parameters to VG20's two, so it has strictly more child-specific structure to recover from the retained observation. `p_loo` of 745 on 1,416 points is the same fact seen from the other side — more than half the data points as effective parameters is what a hierarchical model with roughly one observation per group looks like, and it is precisely the regime in which PSIS is known to break down.

`kfold_loso.py` settles both at once, which is why it is the named adjudicator rather than a nicety: it **refits**, so no importance sampling is involved and Pareto `k` does not arise, and it leaves out a **whole child**, so it asks how well the model predicts a new child rather than a familiar child's next visit. Until it runs, the honest statement is that VG19 fits these data better, not that it predicts better.

**And a smaller margin than the elpd suggests, once the direction is checked.** [202608220748](202608220748-vg19-individual-trajectories.md) §4 works out the between-child spread in spoken words under both models and finds the disagreement **reverses** at about 48 months: VG20's correlated-but-constant effects give the wider individual band below that age, VG19's growing-but-uncorrelated effects above it. Neither model dominates on the quantity the report publishes, so even a confirmed LOO win would not make VG19 the safer basis for individual prediction across the reported range.

On **understood** the two models barely disagree. VG19's curve is a shallow parabola with its minimum at 48.2 months [36.5, 64.9] and VG20's flat 0.787 sits inside VG19's 89% interval at every age in the table. That is consistent with the small `+2.75 SE`, and with `tau_subj_u_1` being the one parameter in the fit that samples poorly.

On **`q`** they disagree substantially. VG19 puts the narrowest between-child spread at 25.3 months [21.4, 28.6] and has it grow 2.6-fold from there to 84 months, passing VG20's constant at around 40 months and ending at two and a half times it. This is the claim that earns the spoken-outcome elpd, and it is a claim with direct reporting consequences: under VG20 a prediction interval for an individual child's comprehension-to-production ratio is the same width at 6 years as at 2, and under VG19 it is much wider. Which of those two a family or a teacher is shown is not a modelling detail.

**Neither model is a superset of the other, and the disagreement is in the reported quantity, so this cannot be settled by choosing between them.** The successor is the 4x4 that carries both the slopes and `rho_uq` — six correlations, unregistered, needing its own Gate 1. Until it exists, VG20 remains the model of record and the age-varying `q` spread is a finding about the data, not a published interval.

Nominal is 0.89 and 0.50. **VG19 has the best-calibrated recovery of any model in the project on both intervals**, and its 50% coverage is essentially nominal where every other model's is 10-20 points short. That is worth stating carefully: recovery under `--truth posterior` simulates from the model's own graph, so this is self-consistency and not a test of mis-specification. What it establishes is that the shortfall recorded in [#225](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/225) and [#229](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/229) is **not uniform across the family** — the model with the richest child structure is the one that recovers best, which is the opposite of what "a richer model is harder to identify" would predict, and it makes a mis-specification contribution to that shortfall more plausible than a pure engine defect. The clean follow-up is the mirror of `vg10_under_vg20_truth`: fit VG20 to data simulated from VG19 and see whether its coverage degrades further.

The comprehension slope, and **both** correlations, recover in 3 of 3. The one parameter that does not is `tau_subj_q_1`, the production-ratio rate — and it misses **downward in the two replicates where it misses at all**, with `tau_subj_q_0` biased upward alongside it. That pairing is the signature of an intercept-rate trade-off: the fit compensates for too small a rate with too large an offset at the reference age.

**The direction is what matters, and it runs the right way.** `tau_subj_q_1` is the parameter carrying the divergence result in [202608220748](202608220748-vg19-individual-trajectories.md) §3, and the engine under-recovers it. The fitted 0.640 is therefore, if anything, an under-estimate: the fan in the production ratio is at least as wide as reported and probably wider, and the between-child spread at 36 months is somewhat over-stated. Both errors compress the reported fan rather than manufacture it. Had the bias run the other way the divergence finding would have had to be withdrawn.

**G3 passes.** The gate asked whether the slope parameters recover; the comprehension rate and both correlations do, the production-ratio rate is biased but conservatively so, and the model's overall calibration is the best available. With three replicates this is indicative rather than SBC, as the pooled row itself says.

## 5. What this does and does not license

It **does** establish that children differ in their own rate of change, on both outcomes, in a model that converges cleanly and is nested at the model of record. It **does not** establish that VG19 should replace anything: VG19 was gated against VG10, not VG20, and by [202608211500](202608211500-vg19-registration.md) §2 the two are not composable — VG19 sets `rho_uq` to zero, which VG20 estimates at +0.368 with an interval excluding zero. A model of record carrying the slope is the 4x4 that neither model is, and it needs its own Gate 1.
