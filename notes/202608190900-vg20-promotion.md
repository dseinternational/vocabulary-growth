# VG20 replaces VG10 as the model of record

**2026-08-19.** VG20 — VG10 plus one free correlation `rho_uq` between a child's comprehension and production-ratio random intercepts — becomes the model of record for the Down syndrome joint understood + spoken estimands, on the study owner's instruction. VG10 becomes a development step.

**Three of the four gates in [#224](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/224) pass; gate 2 does not**, and that is recorded here rather than smoothed over. `rho_uq` itself recovers cleanly in all three replicates — the specific question gate 2 was written to answer is answered — but two replicates missed the convergence threshold and the one assessable replicate under-covers across the full target set, which cannot be attributed to VG20 or to the engine without a baseline that has never been run. One is running (§5). The promotion proceeds because VG20 differs from VG10 by a single parameter: if the under-coverage is the engine's, it is equally VG10's, and the promotion neither creates nor worsens it.

This note records the evidence, what the change does and does not move, one defect the gates caught that would otherwise have retired the model, and two findings that belong to other issues.

## 1. The four gates

| Gate | Question                            | Result                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Does VG20 converge at `rep`?        | **Pass.** 0 divergences, max R-hat 1.0038, min ESS 1,745, BFMI 0.459–0.502 on all six chains. Both tiers clear.                                                                  |
| 2    | Is `rho_uq` recoverable?            | **Not clean. `rho_uq` recovers in 3 of 3 replicates; the run does not clear the gate as posed.** See §5.                                                                         |
| 3    | Does anything move that should not? | **Pass.** Population-level unmoved on 382 of 382 checks; understood child-level unmoved (width ratio 0.986); spoken child-level widens (1.137), which is the correction working. |
| 4    | Does an independent model agree?    | **Pass, with a qualification.** VG16's cross-lag implies about +0.24 on the correlation scale against VG20's +0.368. Overlapping, and the difference is informative — see §4.    |

VG20's convergence is at least as good as the model it replaces, and on one criterion better: VG10's `rep` fit carries **one divergent transition** and so fails the soft tier, while VG20 has none. Both clear the hard tier. Energy is unchanged rather than improved (BFMI 0.470–0.518 on VG10 against 0.459–0.502 on VG20), which is the expected result — the energy behaviour of this family is structural and a single correlation parameter does not move it. The point worth recording is the negative one: **adding a parameter did not cost anything in sampling**, which is not the usual outcome and is consistent with the independence constraint having been a constraint the data were pushing against.

The two fits are on identical data — same analysis-frame hash `sha256:8fcc50ff…`, 1,431 rows, 767 children, 987 understood and 1,428 spoken observations — so every comparison below is like-for-like rather than approximately so.

## 2. What the promotion moves, and what it does not

**Population level: nothing.** Expected words understood (13 ages), expected words spoken (14 ages) and the production ratio `q` (355 grid points) all sit inside VG10's own 89% interval — 382 of 382. The largest movement is 5.97 words on spoken, against intervals hundreds of words wide. This is required rather than reassuring: a correlation between two mean-zero deviations cannot move their mean.

**Comprehension at child level: nothing.** The subject-marginal width ratio is 0.986. This is the sharp check. The Cholesky construction `delta_q = tau_q (rho z1 + sqrt(1-rho^2) z2)` preserves each deviation's marginal SD and changes only their joint behaviour, so if comprehension had widened, the whitening term would have been wrong and `tau_subj_q` silently rescaled.

**Spoken at child level: this is the change.** Spoken is `p_U · q`, so a positive correlation compounds — a child above average on comprehension also tends to convert more of it to speech.

| Age (mo) | VG10 width | VG20 width | Ratio    |
| -------- | ---------- | ---------- | -------- |
| 12       | 3.2        | 4.3        | **1.33** |
| 18       | 13.3       | 17.5       | 1.31     |
| 24       | 40.9       | 52.3       | 1.28     |
| 30       | 91.5       | 113.8      | 1.24     |
| 36       | 166.4      | 202.2      | 1.22     |
| 48       | 333.1      | 382.2      | 1.15     |
| 60       | 427.5      | 477.7      | 1.12     |
| 72       | 461.7      | 509.9      | 1.10     |
| 84       | 473.9      | 516.0      | 1.09     |
| 90       | 477.1      | 518.8      | **1.09** |

The gradient has a clean explanation: comprehension saturates with age, so `p_U → 1` and the spoken deviation approaches the `q` deviation alone, leaving less room for the two to compound. The correction is therefore largest exactly where the counts are smallest and a family is most likely to be told what to expect.

**Between-child DS-versus-TD contrasts: superseded.** `comparison.child_spread_product` integrated `sigmoid(f_u + tau_u Z1)·sigmoid(h + tau_q Z2)` over _independent_ `Z1`, `Z2`, because no DS model estimated a correlation. It now takes an optional `rho` and applies the same Cholesky to the quadrature nodes. Measured on VG20 against VG10:

| Age (mo) | τ_DS spoken, VG10 | τ_DS spoken, VG20 |
| -------- | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| 8        | 1.473             | 1.692             |
| 12       | 1.459             | 1.677             |
| 24       | 1.372             | 1.570             |
| 30       | 1.306             | 1.495             |
| 60       | 0.928             | 1.067             |

About **15% on the logit scale at every age**, with comprehension unaffected (0.797 against 0.784, two independent fits of the same parameter). Every published DS-versus-TD spoken between-child contrast understated the Down syndrome side by that much. The direction is the conservative one — DS was already the more variable side, so the correction widens an existing gap rather than reversing it — and the comparison book already disclosed the assumption and predicted this sign. What it also does is repair the reading the book said was not permitted: the spoken and comprehension ratios can now be set against each other, because the bias that sat on only one of them is gone.

## 3. The defect the gates caught

Gate 3 was written and committed **before any VG20 fit existed** (`8f84703`), with the pass criteria and their _directions_ fixed in advance. The reason is in the criteria themselves: #224's own text says "the reported trajectories should be unchanged" and treats movement as a red flag, but a positive `rho_uq` **should** move one family of quantities. Run without that distinction drawn first, a correct result reads as a failure.

It did. The first run of gate 3 returned a spoken width ratio of **0.9856** — no change. The natural conclusion is _real but immaterial, so not worth adopting_, which is precisely the argument that retired VG16's cross-lag.

It was wrong. `sample_posterior_predictive` drew the unseen child's two deviations as two independent `pm.Normal`s, so the model estimated `rho_uq` and then discarded it when constructing the one quantity the parameter was added to change. Fixed in `dd70ef8` and refitted; the ratio is 1.1365. `scripts/regenerate_plots.py` could not apply the fix — it re-runs the plot stage alone against the _stored_ posterior predictive — which is why the refit was needed and why the corrected numbers took a further 28 minutes rather than a replot.

Two things made the catch possible, and both are worth keeping: writing the criteria before the fit existed, and stating for each criterion whether it passes by moving or by staying put.

## 4. Gate 4, and a correction to how it was first read

VG16 regresses a child's current production-ratio logit on their _earlier_ comprehension residual, `beta_lag` = **+0.203** [0.093, 0.316]. That predictor is an observed residual — persistent standing plus occasion movement plus sampling noise — with reliability `tau_subj_u²/var(x_lag)` = 0.526 [0.465, 0.598], so under classical errors-in-variables attenuation the disattenuated coefficient is **0.383** [0.173, 0.610] ([`202608151120`](202608151120-vg16-cross-lag-quantified.md) §5).

0.383 against VG20's 0.368 looks like a striking agreement. **It is not a like-for-like comparison and was initially recorded as one here.** `x_lag` is not standardised: `beta_lag` is a regression slope in logit units, and `rho_uq` is a correlation. Converting properly, with `tau_subj_u` ≈ 0.797 and `tau_subj_q` ≈ 1.283:

- VG16 disattenuated, on the correlation scale (× `tau_u/tau_q`): **+0.239** [0.110, 0.377]
- VG20 `rho_uq`: **+0.368** [0.287, 0.447]
- Equivalently, as slopes: VG16 **0.383**, VG20 `rho·tau_q/tau_u` = **0.603** [0.460, 0.750]

The intervals overlap and the sign agrees, so the gate passes — but VG20's estimate is about 1.5× VG16's, not equal to it. That difference is evidence, not noise, and it points the same way as §7: VG16's predictor comes from a **different administration**, so it is immune to the within-questionnaire reporter artefact that inflates VG20. The reporter-bias-free estimate is the lower one.

A cleaner internal check needs no scale conversion at all. The correlation of the _realised_ per-child deviations across the 767 children:

| Model | Structure                            | Realised correlation      |
| ----- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| VG10  | independent priors, no cross-lag     | +0.151 [0.106, 0.195]     |
| VG16  | independent priors, cross-lag fitted | +0.134 [0.088, 0.180]     |
| VG20  | correlation estimated                | **+0.371** [0.306, 0.433] |

VG20's realised value reproduces its fitted `rho_uq` (0.369) almost exactly — the parameter does what it claims. VG10's is less than half that, and the difference is the pull of a prior asserting zero, not a difference between the children. **VG10's own output contained the evidence against VG10's assumption**, which is the cleanest single argument for the promotion.

## 5. Gate 2: the parameter recovers, the run does not clear the gate

Three replicates, `test` tier, truth drawn from the model of record's posterior. **The gate as posed is not met**, and the two halves of that have to be reported separately because they point in different directions.

**`rho_uq` itself recovers, in all three replicates.** The truth falls inside the 89% interval every time, with no directional bias, and the posterior SD reproduces the model of record's:

| Replicate | Truth | Posterior median | Posterior SD | z     | Truth inside 89%? |
| --------- | ----- | ---------------- | ------------ | ----- | ----------------- |
| r01       | 0.317 | 0.335            | 0.049        | +0.36 | yes               |
| r02       | 0.478 | 0.440            | 0.044        | −0.87 | yes               |
| r03       | 0.311 | 0.383            | 0.048        | +1.49 | yes               |

Signs alternate, magnitudes are unremarkable, and the SD of 0.044–0.049 matches the 0.050 in the fit of record. On the specific question this gate exists to ask — _can this design identify the parameter the model was added to estimate?_ — the answer is yes.

**The run around it does not clear the gate.** Two of the three replicates missed the convergence threshold (max R-hat 1.011 and 1.012 against 1.010), so the harness declines to assess them at all, and the one assessable replicate covers **0.779** of its 131 targets at the 89% level and 0.412 at the 50% — under-coverage on both. Thirteen quantities are marked not recovered in that replicate, `tau_subj_q` among them.

**A third thing, which is not about VG20 and is the most interesting.** `tau_subj_u` sits below its truth in all three replicates — z = −1.94, −1.24, −1.58, with the truth in the 0.970th, 0.896th and 0.940th quantile of the posterior. Three of three in the same direction is weak on sign alone, but the magnitudes are consistent and this is the **same direction** as [#225](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/225)'s finding that `tau_subject` comes back biased low on VG12. VG12 carries `subject_variance_partition` and VG20 does not, so if this replicates it says the between-child scale is biased low **without** the partition being the cause — which narrows #229's option 1 considerably. Three replicates cannot establish that; it is a lead, and it belongs to #225 rather than to this note.

**Attribution is the open question.** Under-coverage across the whole target set could be a VG20 problem or a property of this engine at `test` tier, and the two have opposite implications. VG10 has never had a recovery run _as registered_ — its own report says so — so there is no baseline to attribute against. One is running at the same tier and replicate count; until it reports, the honest statement is that **gate 2 confirms `rho_uq` and leaves the surrounding calibration unresolved**.

What this does not do is undercut the promotion. VG20 differs from VG10 by one parameter, and if the under-coverage is the engine's it is VG10's too — in which case the promotion neither creates nor worsens it. What it does mean is that gate 2 must be reported as **not passed**, and the calibration question stays open against the baseline.

## 6. Predictive accuracy is suggestive, not decisive

Paired PSIS-LOO on the same observations, pointwise differences (the SEs printed in each fit's `loo_summary.csv` are for each model separately and cannot give the SE of a difference):

| Outcome          | ELPD difference, VG20 − VG10 | SE of the difference | Ratio |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------- | ----- |
| Words spoken     | **+12.66**                   | 6.64                 | +1.91 |
| Words understood | −0.30                        | 5.48                 | −0.06 |
| Combined         | +12.35                       | 8.60                 | +1.44 |

The gain is on spoken and only on spoken, which is where theory says the correlation can matter, and understood is flat as criterion 2 requires. But 1.9 standard errors is suggestive, not decisive, and both models have many high Pareto-k observations (258 and 256 bad, 30 very bad on spoken), so PSIS is not reliable here anyway.

**The case for the promotion does not rest on ELPD.** It rests on the structural argument — VG10 asserts an independence its own fitted deviations contradict — and on the child-level intervals being right, which is a calibration question rather than a predictive-ranking one.

## 7. The caveat that travels with `rho_uq`

Of the 767 children in the DS bivariate pool, **432 are seen once**. Of those, **342 have both outcomes on their single administration** and so inform `rho_uq` only through the agreement between two counts on one questionnaire completed by one parent. (The other 90 have spoken only and contribute essentially nothing.) A parent who over-reports over-reports both, and **shared reporter bias is not separable from shared child ability in that design**.

So `+0.368` leans high, and §4's independent estimate — from a design that reads the two outcomes from different administrations — is 0.239. The honest reading is that the true between-child correlation is positive, materially so, and more likely in the lower half of VG20's interval than the upper. The 15% between-child correction in §2 is correspondingly an upper bound on its own size.

This caveat must travel with the correlation wherever it is published. It is not resolvable within this pool; it would need either a second reporter on the same child or a design that separates the two outcomes in time.

## 8. What was changed, and what was deliberately not

Changed:

- `MODEL_REGISTRY` roles in [`docs/models/README.md`](../docs/models/README.md), `HEADLINE_MODELS` in `recovery/spec.py`, and the `headline`/`all` model lists in `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.github/copilot-instructions.md` and the parameter-recovery runbook.
- `comparison.child_spread_product` and `comparison.subject_heterogeneity` — the correlated-quadrature change of §2, with four new tests including a Monte Carlo check of the correlated integral.
- `DS_KEY`, `DISP_DS_KEY` and `JOINT_DS_KEY` in `scripts/compare_ds_td_re.py`; the DS side of `scripts/compare_models.py` (its `_vg10` output stems renamed to `_vg20`, since nothing in the books referenced them); the default model in `scripts/subject_effect_correlation.py`.
- The report chapters that name the model of record or read its artefacts, and the VG10 and VG20 model reports.

Deliberately not changed:

- **VG10 is a development step, not superseded.** The taxonomy reserves "superseded" for a model replaced after a documented structural or data problem, and says a superseded model never supplies a number. VG10's population trajectories are reproduced exactly by VG20 and its lineage role is intact; what is superseded is its child-level output, not the model. This follows the VG14 → VG15 precedent.
- **The VG05 → VG10 lineage tables keep VG10** and gain VG20 as its successor, in the LOO lineage and the convergence-caveats table. A lineage that ends at its own model of record has to include it.
- **VG15 and the typically-developing models are untouched, but VG15 is not exempt and should not be assumed to be.** VG15 carries `use_subject_re_u`, `use_subject_re_q` _and_ `use_subject_re_sign`, all drawn independently — the same structural assumption as VG10, on three child effects rather than two. Measured in its own fitted deviations across the same 767 children:

  | Pair              | Realised correlation        | P(>0) |
  | ----------------- | --------------------------- | ----- |
  | understood × `q`  | **+0.064** [+0.013, +0.114] | 0.976 |
  | understood × sign | +0.025 [−0.029, +0.081]     | 0.766 |
  | `q` × sign        | +0.035 [−0.019, +0.089]     | 0.842 |

  Positive on the pair that matters, but **much weaker than VG10's +0.151 on the same children**, and the two sign pairs are not distinguishable from zero. VG15's `q` is defined against the sign/speech composition rather than plain spoken and its analysis frame differs, so the two are not strictly comparable — but the evidence against independence is materially thinner here than it was for VG10, and a correlated block for VG15 does not follow from this promotion. It needs its own case. VG13 is the live TD candidate and is [#229](https://github.com/dseinternational/vocabulary-growth/issues/229)'s option 3, where the shared-reporter caveat of §7 is the reason it is not a free win.

- **`vg07_vg09_vg10_q_by_age`** still reads VG10. It is a lineage overlay _about_ VG10, and VG20's `q` is identical to VG10's by construction, so adding it would draw a line on top of another.

## 9. One unrelated defect found in passing

The comparison book's method table said the DS dispersion and distributional contrasts came from **VG07**. `DISP_DS_KEY` moved from VG07 to VG10 on **2026-08-05** — correctly, because #164 gave the TD models child random effects and inverted which DS model made κ like-for-like — and the table was never updated. The book therefore told readers for two weeks that a contrast came from a model it did not come from. The published numbers were computed from VG10 and are unaffected; only the description was wrong. Corrected in the same pass, with the history recorded in the book rather than quietly overwritten.
